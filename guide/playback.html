7Recording playback and speed

For devices that support playback, the single-camera view switches from the live picture to recorded footage. A day is chosen, on supported devices from a calendar that marks the days with recordings, a 24-hour timeline is moved to the wanted time, and a recording or an event, such as a detected person or motion, is played from the list. A recording can be played faster at 2×, 4× or 8×. Days and times follow the clock of the device.

Where playback is available

The recording panel appears below the picture of the single-camera view (see Chapter 6, “Single-camera view”) only for devices that support playback. For all other devices, the view shows the live picture and the controls bar only. Playback is reached from three places: the single-camera view itself, the Recordings button in the close-up of a camera on a full-screen wall (see “The full-screen wall of a device”), and the same button on a LiveView Mix wall.

OfferShown when
Recording panel with day, timeline and listThe device was added with a connection method that offers playback; see “What works with which method”. When the device refuses the sign-in, because the account may not read recordings or because the username or password is wrong, the panel is still shown, with This account cannot read recordings on this device. above the timeline.
Button for the recording calendarThe device also keeps a daily index of its recordings; see “The recording calendar”.
Switch Continuous and EventsThe device was added with a connection method that offers an index of events: of detected people, or of motion and alarm inputs; see “What works with which method”. The switch is shown even when the device itself does not answer the search of that index; see “The list of recordings and events”. With any other connection method, the switch appears only when the chosen day or the day before holds a recording that the device made because of an event. Otherwise, only the list of continuous recordings is shown, with no switch.
Speed buttonDuring playback, when the device can play the recording faster. If it cannot, a notice appears; see “Playback speed”.
  • Recorder channel: the recordings of the channel come from the storage of the recorder. The single-camera view of a channel shows the recordings of that channel only.
  • Standalone camera: the recordings come from the storage of the camera itself, for cameras that offer a search for recordings. A camera without recordings shows an empty day. A camera that reports that it keeps no recordings of its own, for example because a recorder records it, shows This camera doesn't keep recordings of its own. If a recorder records it, play the footage back from the recorder. in place of the list.
  • The screen and the controls are the same for both. A recorder and a camera differ only in where the recordings are kept and in whether the device supports playback.
  • Playback is offered by devices that are connected through their own protocol or through ONVIF, when the connection supports it. Which brands and connection methods offer playback is listed in Chapter 16, “Supported brands and connection methods”.
  • A device that was added through ONVIF shows the recording panel, but finds recordings only when it offers the ONVIF services for searching and replaying recordings. Otherwise, every day shows an empty list.
  • Some recorders that are reached through ONVIF list only the recordings of about the last 24 hours. Older days then show No continuous recordings on this day., although the recorder still keeps the footage. Such footage can be played on the recorder itself.
  • A device that was added through a brand from the list provides live view only, without playback; see “Choosing the connection method”. The sample cameras and YouTube and Twitch streams contain no recordings.

Choosing a day

Prerequisite: the single-camera view of a camera that supports playback is open; see “Opening the view”. Below the controls bar, the heading Recordings shows the current day on its right, with the timeline below it and the list of recordings below the timeline.

  1. Tap the day on the right of the heading Recordings.

    Check: A calendar opens.

  2. Tap the wanted day.

    Check: The calendar closes, and the heading shows the new day. Loading recordings… appears briefly, then the timeline and the list show the recordings of that day. The picture does not change.

  • The cursor of the timeline starts at the current time of the device for today, and at 12:00 for any other day.
  • Choosing a day does not start playback by itself. Playback starts from the timeline or from the list.
  • The timeline shows the chosen day and the day before it, so that footage across midnight can be reached.
  • Any day can be chosen, including days without recordings. Such a day shows an empty list. To see which days have recordings, use the recording calendar where the device offers it.

The clock of the device

Days and times in playback are those of the device, not of the iPhone. For devices that report their time zone, "today" and "now" are the date and time of the device, also when the iPhone is in another time zone: seen from a place where it is still yesterday, the day picker opens on the day of the device and the cursor on its current time. The right day is also found when the iPhone uses another calendar than the Gregorian one. For devices that do not report a time zone, the time zone of the iPhone is used.

The recording calendar

On devices that keep a daily index of their recordings, a calendar button sits between the heading Recordings and the day. Its calendar marks the days that have recordings, so that a day does not have to be tried one after another. Without such an index, the button is not shown and the day is chosen as described in “Choosing a day”.

  1. Tap the calendar button (Recording calendar) next to the day.

    Check: The sheet Recording calendar opens on the month of the chosen day. A spinner next to the name of the month shows while the device is asked. Then the days with recordings appear in bold with a dot below them, and the line Days with a dot have recordings. appears below the month. The chosen day is in a filled circle. Days after today on the clock of the device are dimmed and cannot be chosen.

  2. Tap one of the arrows beside the name of the month (Previous month or Next month).

    Check: The calendar shows the other month and marks its days with recordings. The arrow to the next month is unavailable in the current month of the device.

  3. Tap a day.

    Check: The sheet closes, and the day is chosen as with the day picker: Loading recordings… appears, then the timeline and the list show the recordings of that day.

  • Tap Done to close the sheet without choosing a day.
  • Each month is asked once while the sheet is open.
  • The week starts on the day set by the region of the iPhone. Month and weekday names follow the language of the app.

Using the timeline

The timeline shows the recordings of the chosen day along a ruler. The orange cursor stays in the middle, and the ruler moves under it. All times are the clock of the device, not the time zone of the iPhone; see “Choosing a day”.

ElementMeaning
Orange line with a bubble above itThe cursor, fixed in the middle. The bubble shows the time at the cursor, and the date as well while the cursor is on the day before the chosen day.
Blue barsContinuous recordings.
Red barsRecordings that the device made because of an event. They are listed under Events as Event recording.
Thin red marksThe start of each event in the Events list: a detected person, motion, an alarm input, or a recording that the device made because of an event. The marks are shown whichever list is selected.
Dark gapsNo recording exists at that time.

Hour labels below the barThe scale. The divisions become finer when the timeline is enlarged.
Two magnifier buttons at the rightZoom in (plus) and Zoom out (minus) change the span that the timeline shows.

  1. Touch the timeline and drag sideways.

    Check: The ruler moves under the cursor, and the time in the bubble changes. The picture stays live while the finger moves.

  2. Lift the finger when the cursor is at the wanted time.

    Check: The picture switches to the recording from that time. The badge turns red and shows PLAYBACK 15:00:03 with the time of the playback, and Back to Live replaces the SD/HD switch.

  3. Tap Zoom in (the plus magnifier), or spread two fingers apart on the timeline.

    Check: The timeline shows a shorter span, down to 10 minutes. The button is dimmed at that limit.

  4. Tap Zoom out (the minus magnifier), or bring two fingers together on the timeline.

    Check: The timeline shows a longer span, up to 48 hours (both days). The button is dimmed at that limit. The timeline opens with a span of 24 hours.

  • A drag shorter than about 6 points on the screen is ignored, so that touching the timeline does not leave the live picture by accident.
  • When the finger is lifted in a gap, the next recording after the gap plays. The badge and the cursor jump to the start of that recording. When no recording follows, the message No recording at the selected time. appears with Retry.
  • While a recording plays, the cursor follows the playback by itself. While the timeline is being touched, the cursor stays where the finger puts it.
  • With VoiceOver, the timeline is one element: swiping up or down moves the cursor, and a double tap plays from there.

The list of recordings and events

Below the timeline, a list shows the recordings of the chosen day. When the device offers events (see “Where playback is available”), a switch above the list selects Continuous or Events.

  • Continuous: one row per continuous recording, newest first. Each row shows a blue dot, the start time and the length of the recording in hours, minutes and seconds. A row of the day before starts with the date. The row of the recording that is playing is highlighted and shows a speaker symbol. Recordings that the device made because of an event are not in this list.
  • Events: one row per event of the chosen day, newest first. Each row shows a red symbol and a title for the kind of event, the start time below the title, and a play symbol at the right. An event that began on the day before starts with the date. Events of the same kind that follow each other within 2 seconds are merged into one row. The row of the event that is playing is highlighted and shows a speaker symbol.
Row under EventsSymbolMeaning

Where the connection method offers no index of events, the list under Events holds only the recordings that the device made because of an event.

Playing a recording from the list

  1. Tap a row under Continuous.

    Check: The picture switches to that recording, from its start. The row is highlighted, and the cursor jumps to the start time.

Playing an event

  1. Tap Events above the list.

    Check: The list shows the events of the chosen day, for example Person detected or Motion detected.

  2. Tap a row.

    Check: Playback starts about 5 seconds before the event, or at the start of the recording when the recording began later. The row is highlighted, and the badge shows the playback time.

Which kinds of event a device lists is shown in “What works with which method”. Other kinds of detection, for example vehicles, are not searched. Cam Hub reads at most about 2,000 recordings and 2,000 detected people per channel and day; anything beyond that is not listed.

While a recording plays

  • Badge: the red badge at the top left of the picture shows the playback time on the clock of the device, and the time moves with the picture. It matches the time that the camera prints into its picture, when the camera does so. When the playback time is on the day before the chosen day, the date precedes the time.
  • Continuing: when a recording file ends, playback continues into the next recording by itself, so that an evening can be watched without a stop at every file. Cam Hub looks for newer recordings first, because the device may still be writing.
  • Sound: the speaker button works at normal speed only; see “Snapshot, sound and quality”.
  • Snapshot: takes the current live picture of the device, not a frame of the recording.
  • Clip: the record button records the recording as it plays, to send it or save it in the photo library; see “Recording a clip”.
  • Zoom and pan: work as in live mode; see “Zoom and pan”. The camera control for PTZ and the talk button are hidden during playback.
  • Refused recording: when the recorder does not play a recording, for example because it was overwritten, the picture shows The recorder would not play this recording. It may have been overwritten — choose another time. Choose another time on the timeline.
  • Recordings that arrive as a file: some devices deliver a recording as a file. The picture area then shows Downloading recording… 45% with a progress bar, and the recording plays in the video player of the system, which has its own controls. The timeline cursor does not follow in that case. The downloaded files are kept in a cache of up to 500 MB on the iPhone, so that the same recording does not have to be downloaded again; Delete All Data removes the cache, and deleting the device removes the files downloaded from it. In place of the record button, the share button (Share Recording) offers the whole file; see “Recording a clip”.
  • Background: when the app leaves the foreground or the iPhone is locked, playback stops. When the app returns, playback resumes at the point it had reached.

Returning to the live picture

  1. Tap Back to Live in the controls bar.

    Check: The live picture returns with the quality that was used before. The badge shows LIVE, the day changes to today on the clock of the device, the cursor moves to the current time of the device, and the speed returns to 1×.

At the end of the last recording

When the playback passes the end of the last recording, the last picture stays on the screen, and a card with End of recording and Back to Live appears. This is not an error.

  1. Tap Back to Live on the card.

    Check: The live picture returns as described above.

Playback speed

The speed button is a round button at the lower right of the picture. It shows the current speed, 1× at normal speed. Each tap selects the next of four speeds, in the order 1×, 2×, 4×, 8× and back to 1×. The button is shown only during playback and only when the recording can be played faster; it appears a moment after the recording starts. Live pictures always run at normal speed.

  1. Start a recording, as described in “Using the timeline” or “The list of recordings and events”.

    Check: The speed button at the lower right of the picture shows 1×.

  2. Tap the speed button.

    Check: The button shows 2× and is tinted. The picture reopens at the current position, and the time advances twice as fast. About one second of the picture is lost when the stream reopens.

  3. Tap the speed button again.

    Check: The button shows 4×, and the time advances four times as fast.

  4. Tap the speed button again.

    Check: The button shows 8×, and the time advances eight times as fast.

  5. Tap the speed button again.

    Check: The button shows 1×, and the recording plays at normal speed.

  • The speed stays selected when another recording or another time is chosen during the same playback. Back to Live returns it to 1×.
  • Sound plays at 1× only. Above 1×, the picture runs without sound.
  • A higher speed makes the device send the picture faster, which uses more bandwidth.
  • The button is not shown for devices that stream recordings in a way that does not allow a change of speed.
  • In landscape orientation, the speed button is part of the strip over the picture; see “Playback in landscape”. The notice for a device that cannot play faster is not shown there; the button only returns to 1×.
A device that cannot play faster

Not every device accepts a faster speed. When the device cannot play the recording faster, the notice This recorder can't play faster than normal speed. appears above the button for about 4 seconds, and the button returns to 1×. The notice can take a few seconds to appear, because Cam Hub tries several ways of requesting the faster speed before it gives up. The same happens when the device accepts the request but the picture does not run faster. The recording continues at normal speed.

Playback in landscape

Prerequisite: the rotation lock of the iPhone is off. On an iPad, the layout does not change with the orientation; the steps below apply to the iPhone. On a half-folded iPhone Duo, the timeline stays on the lower half; see “The view on a folded iPhone Duo”.

  1. Start a recording and turn the iPhone sideways.

    Check: The picture fills the screen. A strip over the lower edge of the picture holds the badge PLAYBACK 15:00:03, the speed button, Back to Live and the timeline.

  2. Drag the timeline in the strip and lift the finger at the wanted time.

    Check: The picture plays the recording from that time, as in upright orientation.

  3. Leave the strip untouched for about 5 seconds.

    Check: While the cursor follows the playback, the strip and the buttons at the top of the picture stay on screen. When the cursor stands still, for example at the end of the last recording or while a recording downloads or plays in the video player of the system, they fade out after about 5 seconds. A tap on the picture shows them again, except while a recording downloads or plays in the video player of the system: the controls then return when the iPhone is turned upright.

  4. Tap Back to Live in the strip, or turn the iPhone upright again.

    Check: The live picture returns, or the upright layout with the recording panel is shown again.

The strip appears only during playback, because a live picture has nothing to scrub. The day, the recording calendar, the list of recordings, Snapshot and the SD/HD switch are available in upright orientation only. The record button is at the top right of the picture; see “Recording a clip”.

What is not available

  • Playback for devices that were added through a brand from the list, for the sample cameras and for YouTube and Twitch streams; see “Where playback is available”.
  • A recording calendar for devices that keep no daily index of their recordings.
  • A search for kinds of detection other than people, motion and alarm inputs, for example vehicles.
  • A timeline that spans more than the chosen day and the day before it.
  • Deleting recordings. To keep or send a part of a recording, record it as a clip of up to 10 minutes; see “Recording a clip”. A snapshot always shows the current live picture.
  • A choice between SD and HD during playback, slow motion, reverse playback, and speeds other than 1×, 2×, 4× and 8×.
  • Sound at speeds above 1×.
  • A pause button, frame-by-frame stepping, jumps of a few seconds, a box for entering a time, buttons for the next or previous recording, and a free-text search.
  • Playback on Apple TV is described in Chapter 14, “Apple TV”.
