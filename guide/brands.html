16Supported brands and connection methods

This is the only chapter that names brands. It lists the connection methods of the add-device form, what each one makes possible, what a device must offer, and what to prepare on a device before it is added. Everything else in this manual describes functions without naming a brand.

Connection methods

The list Advanced › Connect using in the add-device form starts with four methods: Auto-detect, ONVIF, Xiongmai (XMEye) and RTSP. Below them, the section Brands lists 71 brands in alphabetical order, with a search field that also finds a brand by other names under which its cameras are sold. Hikvision, Dahua and Synology are not in the list: Auto-detect finds them with their own protocols. The form itself is described in “The Add Device form” and the choice in “Choosing the connection method”.

ChoiceWhat Cam Hub doesSuitable forAccount
Auto-detect (default) First checks, without signing in, which ports are open at the address. Then tries, in this order: on the main web port, a brand recognized there, then the protocol of Hikvision, the protocol of Dahua and the programming interface of Synology Surveillance Station; one of these three recognized on another port, for example the web port 5000 of a Synology NAS; Xiongmai's own protocol on port 34567, at local addresses only; ONVIF on each port where it answered; and plain RTSP on each RTSP port that answered, with the paths of a brand that the device revealed, then the paths that most cameras use, then the paths of every brand in the list. The first way that connects is kept, with its port. A refused sign-in from Hikvision, Dahua, Synology or Xiongmai ends the attempt at once; ONVIF and RTSP end it after the second refusal. A port typed under Advanced is tried first, and a typed port other than 80 or 554 that is open is used alone. Most devices: cameras and recorders of Hikvision, Dahua and Synology, Xiongmai devices in the local network, any device with ONVIF, and cameras that serve RTSP at a common path or at a path of a brand in the list. The account of the device. For Synology: an account of the network storage device that may use Surveillance Station.
ONVIF Skips the detection and uses only the open ONVIF protocol, on the port in HTTP Port, or on the HTTPS port when Use HTTPS is on. Cam Hub looks for the service at /onvif/device_service and, when that address does not answer, at /onvif/service and /onvif/device. The video is fetched at the address that the device announces. Any camera or recorder with ONVIF; also a Hikvision, Dahua or Synology device that is to be used over ONVIF. The ONVIF user of the device. Where the device keeps a separate list of ONVIF users, a user has to exist in that list.
Xiongmai (XMEye) Uses Xiongmai's own protocol (XMEye / NetSurveillance) on the port in Device Port, 34567 unless it was changed or forwarded. Any address is accepted, also one outside the local network; for such an address the form warns that the protocol does not encrypt the video and that many devices protect the password only weakly. Xiongmai cameras and recorders that Auto-detect does not find, that use another port, or that are reached from outside the local network. The account of the device.
RTSP Connects over plain RTSP and tries the paths that most cameras use, or the path entered. No control interface of the device is contacted. Live view only. A camera of any brand with ONVIF switched off, when its brand is not in the list. The camera account of the device.
A brand from the list (see “The brands in the list”) Connects over plain RTSP, at the stream paths that the maker documents. No control interface of the device is contacted. Live view only. Devices of that brand when ONVIF is switched off or does not work, and recorders of that brand that offer only RTSP. The camera account of the device; for five of the brands a separate account.

Auto-detect is the starting point. ONVIF is the next choice when Auto-detect reports Not a supported device., or when a device is to be used over ONVIF on purpose. Xiongmai (XMEye) is the choice for a Xiongmai device that Auto-detect cannot reach. A brand from the list, or RTSP, is the last choice, for a device with ONVIF switched off. A Xiongmai recorder that reports that it encrypts its video cannot be watched over Xiongmai's own protocol; Auto-detect then moves on to ONVIF, and such a recorder is added with ONVIF. The developer has tested selected Hikvision, Dahua and Synology devices. Whether a particular model works, with any method, depends on its firmware, its settings, its stream format, its account rights and the network.

On Apple TV, the add form has no Connect using list and no Brands section. Two switches take their place, and only one of them can be on. Connect via ONVIF corresponds to ONVIF. Connect via Xiongmai corresponds to Xiongmai (XMEye): it replaces HTTP Port and RTSP Port with Device Port, filled in with 34567 when the field still holds 80, removes Use HTTPS, and shows the same warning in orange for an address outside the local network. With both switches off, the form uses Auto-detect. RTSP and the brands from the list cannot be selected on Apple TV; Auto-detect still tries their stream paths and adds such a camera as a single camera.

  • Live view: with ONVIF, the device must announce a stream address; when the device does not name its media service, Cam Hub asks at the usual addresses of that service. Video is fetched over RTSP on TCP, and over UDP when the device refuses TCP.
  • SD and HD: with Synology Surveillance Station, the choice is hidden, and SD uses the low-resolution stream of a camera that has one. With Xiongmai, HD is the main stream and SD the extra stream. With ONVIF, the largest profile of the device is HD and the smallest is SD, and a single profile serves both. With RTSP and the brands from the list, SD uses the second stream that belongs to the path found, where the device serves it; otherwise SD shows the same stream as HD.
  • Recording playback: with Hikvision, a camera with its own storage plays back like a recorder. With Dahua, the recorder's main stream is played, and moving to another time starts the playback again at that moment. With Synology Surveillance Station and Xiongmai, the recording is streamed from the chosen moment. With ONVIF, the device needs the search and replay services that ONVIF defines for playback. Some recorders that are reached through ONVIF list only the recordings of about the last 24 hours.
  • Playback speed: with Hikvision, Dahua and ONVIF, the recorder may refuse a faster speed, and Cam Hub then shows a notice. With Synology Surveillance Station and Xiongmai, streamed playback has no speed button.
  • Calendar: a Hikvision recorder that reports which days hold recordings has those days marked in the calendar of the playback date; see “The recording calendar”.
  • Events list: the switch between Continuous and Events above the list of recordings; see “The list of recordings and events”. With Hikvision, the list shows detected people (Person detected) where the device keeps an index of them, and the recordings that the device made because of an event (Event recording). Synology Surveillance Station lists motion (Motion detected) and alarm inputs (Alarm input). Dahua and Xiongmai list the recordings that the device made because of an event (Event recording); for them, the switch appears only when the chosen day or the day before holds such a recording. With ONVIF, every recording counts as continuous, so there is no Events list.
  • PTZ: with Hikvision, a channel needs PTZ switched on; saved positions appear when the device lists them, and the home position is always available. With Dahua, PTZ must be bound to that channel; saved positions are offered, but there is no home position. With Synology Surveillance Station, the controls follow what the system reports for the camera: four directions, and the home position when it is reported. With Xiongmai, a camera with a head gets directions, zoom and saved positions 1 to 16 by number, without a home position. With ONVIF, the axes follow what the device reports; a camera with zoom only gets no direction pad. See “PTZ control: moving the camera”.
  • Aiming: offered when a Hikvision camera reports that it supports positioning by a point or an area of the picture.
  • Snapshot: with Xiongmai, the picture is taken from the first full frame of the live stream. With ONVIF, the device must give a snapshot address.
  • Clip recording: works with the pictures in H.264 and H.265 that the view plays; see “Recording a clip”. With Synology Surveillance Station, an account without permission to fetch the video stream receives single pictures (MJPEG) instead, and its view has no record button. A recording that Synology Surveillance Station delivers as a file is shared whole.
  • Sound: with Synology Surveillance Station, only G.711 plays over its usual streaming path. Xiongmai devices send G.711.
  • Talking: offered for standalone Hikvision cameras that report a two-way audio channel in G.711; not for the channels of a recorder. See “Two-way talk”.
  • Disk Health: Hikvision reports the state of each disk, with S.M.A.R.T. details where the device provides them. Dahua reports state, capacity and, where available, temperature, but no S.M.A.R.T. details. A Synology NAS is read over SNMP, which must be switched on at the NAS and set up in Cam Hub. See “Disk Health”.
  • Channel list: Hikvision and Dahua list the channels of a recorder with names and signal state, and a device that is not a recorder has one channel. Synology Surveillance Station lists every camera of the system as a channel with its signal state. Xiongmai devices report their number of channels and the names given to them, without a signal state. ONVIF lists one channel for each separate video source and does not report a signal state. RTSP and the brands from the list have no list: the number of cameras is entered (1 to 64), and every channel is shown as online.
  • HTTPS: offered when the device serves HTTPS on its web interface, and turned on for each device; see “Adding a device over HTTPS” and “HTTPS for a saved device”. Xiongmai's own protocol and plain RTSP have no web interface for it. Video over RTSP is not encrypted by it.

Not available with any method: focus and iris control, saving or exporting recordings (apart from a recording that arrives as a file, which can be shared whole), and logging in to a cloud account.

The brands in the list

The brands in the Brands section are connected over plain RTSP. Selecting a brand adds a section named after it, with the field Cameras (1 to 64) and a path field. With 1, the device is added as a camera that is named after the brand; with a larger number, it is added as a recorder with channels CH1 to CHn, and it counts as a recorder for the plan. A blank path field makes Cam Hub try the documented paths in turn and keep the one that the device answers. Only a particular path needs to be entered. The choice RTSP works the same way with the paths that most cameras use.

The 71 brands: 2N, 3S Vision / A-Linking, ACTi, Acumen, AirLive, ANNKE, Apexis / Sumpple, Arecont Vision, Avigilon, AVTECH, Axis, Basler, Beward, Bosch Security, Brickcom, Canon, COMPRO, D-Link, DIGITUS, DynaColor, EDIMAX, eneo, EnGenius, Etrovision, eufy, EverFocus, EZVIZ, FlexWATCH, Foscam, GeoVision, Grandstream, Hanwha Vision (Wisenet), HVIEW, i-PRO (Panasonic), IDIS, Imou, INSTAR, iPUX, IQinVision (IQeye), Jovision, JVC, LILIN, Lorex, Messoa, Milesight, MOBOTIX, NEXCOM, Night Owl, Oncam, Pelco, PiXORD, Reolink, SHANY, Siemens, Sony, Sricam, StarDot, Sunell, Swann, TP-Link (Tapo / VIGI), TRENDnet, TVT, UniFi (Ubiquiti), Uniview (UNV), VIVOTEK, Vstarcam, Wansview, Wyze, Y-cam, YI / Kami, Zavio.

  • Separate account: Axis, TP-Link (Tapo / VIGI), ANNKE, MOBOTIX and 2N need their own camera or ONVIF account, created in the web interface or the app of the maker, before RTSP works. The administrator login alone may be refused.
  • RTSP port: three brands use another default port, which the form fills in when the brand is selected: ACTi 7070, Foscam 88 and Vstarcam 10554.
  • Recorders: a documented recorder form exists for 3S Vision / A-Linking, ANNKE, AVTECH, Beward, DIGITUS, eneo, eufy, GeoVision, Hanwha Vision (Wisenet), IDIS, Imou, Lorex, Milesight, Night Owl, Pelco (encoders), Reolink, Sunell, Swann, TP-Link (Tapo / VIGI) (VIGI recorders), TRENDnet, TVT, Uniview (UNV) and VIVOTEK. For the other brands, the camera paths are used for every channel; only a path that contains a channel number reaches a different camera on each channel.
  • Paths that vary: for many brands, some paths depend on the model or the firmware, so the first attempt may not match. The form shows a note below the path field for these brands.
  • Auto-detect uses the same paths: when no other way connects, Auto-detect tries the paths of every brand in the list by itself and adds the camera as a single camera. Selecting a brand by hand is needed mainly for a recorder, to enter the number of cameras, or to pin one path.

Requirements and limits

TopicRequirement or limit
HTTP and HTTPSCam Hub contacts devices over plain HTTP, or over HTTPS where it is turned on for a device; Xiongmai devices over their own protocol instead. Video is fetched over RTSP, on TCP first and on UDP when the device refuses TCP; HTTPS does not encrypt it. With HTTPS, Cam Hub trusts the certificate that the device presented when HTTPS was turned on, a self-signed one included, or a certificate that a public certificate authority issued for the address. A device with HTTP switched off, or one that accepts HTTPS only, is added with Use HTTPS; see “Adding a device over HTTPS”. Auto-detect finds the HTTP web interface of a network storage device on port 5000 by itself; enter the port in HTTP Port only when the web interface uses another port. With Use HTTPS, Cam Hub also finds its HTTPS port 5001 by itself.
ONVIF switched onFor the choice ONVIF, ONVIF must be switched on at the device. Cam Hub looks for the service at /onvif/device_service, /onvif/service or /onvif/device on the port entered in HTTP Port, or on the HTTPS port when Use HTTPS is on. Some devices have ONVIF switched off in the factory settings.
RTSP switched onVideo is fetched over RTSP. With Auto-detect, Cam Hub finds the RTSP port; otherwise it uses the port entered in RTSP Port (usually 554). A network storage device may use its own streaming path first. Where the device has a setting for RTSP authentication, Digest is the method to select.
A separate ONVIF userSome devices keep a list of ONVIF users that is separate from the users of the web interface, and the administrator login does not work for ONVIF. Create a user in that list. Five brands in the list need their own camera or ONVIF account before RTSP works: Axis, TP-Link (Tapo / VIGI), ANNKE, MOBOTIX and 2N.
Account rightsThe account needs the right to view live video, and for playback the right to read recordings. For Synology, the account must be allowed to use Surveillance Station. Cam Hub has no field for a verification code, so an account that requires two-step verification cannot be used.
Clock of the deviceThe ONVIF sign-in carries a time stamp of the device. A clock that is minutes off makes the sign-in fail. Set the time on the device, for example with network time. The times of recordings are the clock of the recorder.
Same network, no cloudCam Hub connects directly to the address of the device and has no cloud relay. From outside the home network, a device can be reached only through an address that is reachable from the internet, or through a VPN. Devices that work only through the cloud app of their maker cannot be added, and Cam Hub offers no sign-in to a cloud account.
Xiongmai outside the local networkAuto-detect speaks Xiongmai's own protocol only to local addresses. A Xiongmai device behind a forwarded port or a dynamic domain name is added with Xiongmai (XMEye). The protocol does not encrypt the video, and many devices protect the password only weakly. How you reach a device from outside, for example through a VPN, is your own choice.
Video formatsCam Hub plays H.264 and H.265, decoded in hardware by the phone. A phone without hardware support for H.265, and a picture size above the limit of the decoder, give no picture. MJPEG is used only as a fallback for live view with one of the families.
SoundFor listening, G.711 (A-law and µ-law) and AAC-LC are played; any other format is silent. Talking through the camera works only with standalone Hikvision cameras that offer two-way audio in G.711.
Locked accountsAfter several wrong attempts, a device can lock the account, often for about 30 minutes. The port check of Auto-detect sends no sign-in. Cam Hub stops at the first sign-in refused by a brand's own protocol and after the second one refused over ONVIF or RTSP, and the device report sends no further sign-in after a refusal.
Adding a device againThe same address, port and username update the stored device instead of creating a second one; see “The Add Device form”.

What to prepare on the device

The following checklist applies to every brand. Items that the device does not have can be skipped.

  • Activate the device and set an administrator password. A new device must be activated before any app can sign in; see “Activating a new device”.
  • Give the device a fixed address in the home network, in the device or as a reservation in the router. Cam Hub keeps the address that was entered; see “A device is added, but it is not reachable”.
  • Place the phone and the device in the same network, without a guest network, a separate VLAN, client isolation or a VPN between them.
  • Switch on the HTTP service of the device and note its port, usually 80.
  • Switch on RTSP and note its port, usually 554. Select Digest for RTSP authentication where the device offers the choice.
  • Switch on ONVIF where the device has the setting, and create a separate ONVIF or camera user where the device keeps its own list or the brand requires one.
  • Set the date, the time and the time zone, for example with network time.
  • Set the video to H.264 or H.265, at a moderate resolution, with a key frame interval of one to two seconds. Use a sub stream with a lower resolution for SD where the device offers one.
  • Use an account that may view live video and, for playback, read recordings of the channels that are to be shown.
  • For playback, check that the device records and that a disk or card is present.
  • For Disk Health of a Synology NAS, switch on SNMP at the NAS.
  • Note the address, the username and the password, which the add-device form asks for, and the ports in case Auto-detect does not find the device.
Devices that cannot be added

A device that works only through the cloud app of its maker, or offers no connection on the local network, cannot be added to Cam Hub. A device that accepts HTTPS only is added with Use HTTPS; see “Adding a device over HTTPS”.

Activating a new device

A new or reset device of some brands, Hikvision among them, has no administrator password yet. It must be activated before any app can sign in: activation sets the administrator password. Until then, every sign-in is refused, and adding the device fails with Wrong username or password.

The network scan can show that a Hikvision device is not activated yet:

  • When the device answers the scan's question and reports that it is not activated, its row in Found Devices shows Not activated; see “Finding devices with the network scan”.
  • The add-device form opened from that row starts with the notice This Hikvision device has not been activated yet. Activate it first — with Hikvision's SADP tool or the device's own web page — by setting an admin password.
  • A device that does not answer the scan's question shows no such mark, whether it is activated or not.

To activate a device:

  1. Activate the device with the maker's own tool or on the device's own web page, as its documentation describes, and set the administrator password there.

    Check: The device accepts a sign-in with the administrator account and the new password, for example on its web page.

  2. In the add-device form of Cam Hub, enter the administrator account, usually admin, and the new password, then tap Add.

    Check: The form closes and the device appears in the device list.

If adding still fails, see “When adding fails: the checklist”.

The compatibility reference in the app

Path: Devices › ＋ › Add Device › Compatibility reference. The page Compatibility lists what the developer states as supported and not supported.

  • Supported: Hikvision and Dahua, both marked Recommended, then Synology, Xiongmai (XMEye), SoCatch (iWatch) with the note Local-network (LAN) connection, Most RTSP cameras and Most ONVIF cameras.
  • Not supported: the connection of SoCatch (iWatch) over the wide area, because it runs through the remote-access service of its maker, which is closed to other apps. Such cameras are to be added on the local network.
  • The Hikvision entry has the button How to keep it off the internet, with notes on keeping a camera without a gateway, on blocking its internet access, and on the trade-off that such a camera can then be watched only from the network it is on. In some regions, the button is not shown.
  • A note at the bottom states that the names are trademarks of their owners, that Cam Hub is developed independently, and that a successful connection means technical compatibility only.
  • The entry Compatibility reference is not shown in a form that was opened from a scan result that named the brand of the device.
