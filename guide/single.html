6Single-camera view

The single-camera view shows one camera at full width, live or from recordings. It offers zoom and pan, a snapshot, a clip of the picture to share, sound, two-way talk for cameras that support it, the choice between SD and HD and, for cameras with a movable head, PTZ control. For devices that support playback, the view also holds the recording timeline; see Chapter 7, “Recording playback and speed”. On an iPad and on iPhone Duo, a camera can have a window of its own. YouTube and Twitch streams open in a full-screen viewer of their own.

Opening the view

The view opens from four places:

  • the Devices tab: a thumbnail on the device page (see “The device page”);
  • the Live tab: a thumbnail on the wall (see “The Live tab”);
  • the close-up of a camera on a full-screen wall or on a LiveView Mix wall: the Recordings button, shown for devices that support playback (see “The full-screen wall of a device”). The view then opens over the wall, and Done at the top right returns to the close-up;
  • a window of its own on an iPad or on the inner display of iPhone Duo (see “A camera in a window of its own”).

Prerequisite: the channel is enabled, within the plan and online. On the device page, a tap on a paused or disabled thumbnail offers Resume Video or Enable instead of opening the view; see “What a thumbnail shows”. The thumbnail of a YouTube or Twitch stream opens the stream full screen instead; see “YouTube and Twitch streams”.

  1. Tap the Devices tab, then tap the device.

    Check: The device page shows the thumbnails of the channels.

  2. Tap the thumbnail of a channel that shows a live picture.

    Check: The single-camera view opens. The title is the name of the camera, and the green badge LIVE is shown at the top left of the picture. The picture starts with the quality of Settings › Default quality › Single camera (HD unless changed).

Tap the back button in the top-left corner to leave the view.

The picture can be enlarged on the screen. This is a digital enlargement of the picture and works in live mode and during playback. It does not move the lens of the camera; for that, see “PTZ control: moving the camera”.

Snapshot, sound and quality

Taking a snapshot

  1. Tap Snapshot at the left of the controls bar.

    Check: The sheet Snapshot slides up. It shows a progress indicator and then the picture.

  2. Tap Share.

    Check: The sharing sheet of the system opens. From there, the picture can be saved, copied or sent with the actions that the system offers. Save Image puts it in the photo library; the first time, the system asks whether Cam Hub may add to the photo library (see “Permission requests and system dialogs”).

  3. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The sheet closes.

  • The snapshot is a current picture that Cam Hub requests from the device. It is taken from the live picture of the device also while a recording plays, and it is not a frame of the recording.
  • When the device does not supply a snapshot, the sheet closes again and the message Couldn't take a snapshot. Check the connection to the camera and try again. appears. Tap OK to close it. Some devices cannot supply a snapshot at all, for example a device that was added through a brand from the list.

Switching the sound on and off

The speaker button appears only when the camera sends sound in a format that Cam Hub can play. A camera without sound, or with an unsupported sound format, has no button.

  1. Tap the speaker button next to Snapshot (Unmute).

    Check: The symbol changes from a crossed-out speaker to a speaker with sound waves, and the sound of the camera plays.

  2. Tap the speaker button again (Mute).

    Check: The symbol shows the crossed-out speaker again, and the sound stops.

  • The speaker plays the sound of the camera. To speak through the camera, see “Two-way talk”.
  • The sound plays even when the ring/silent switch of the iPhone is set to silent.
  • The choice is one switch for the whole app. It is remembered and is off at first. Thumbnails and walls never play sound; only the single-camera view, the close-up of a camera and the full-screen viewer of a YouTube or Twitch stream do (see “YouTube and Twitch streams”).
  • While camera sound plays, other audio on the iPhone is lowered. Sound stops when the app leaves the foreground.
  • During playback, sound plays at normal speed only; see “Playback speed”.

Switching between SD and HD

  1. Tap SD or HD in the switch at the right of the controls bar.

    Check: The stream restarts, Connecting… shows briefly, and the picture continues with the selected quality. The change is not stored.

The switch is shown only in live mode and only when the device offers two streams for the camera. During playback, Back to Live takes its place. The starting quality comes from Settings › Default quality › Single camera. HD is sharper and uses more bandwidth and more resources of the iPhone. For devices that provide one stream per channel, SD and HD are the same picture. The rules for all screens are in “SD and HD on device screens”.

Recording a clip

The record button saves what the view shows as a video, live or from a recording, so that a moment can be sent to someone or kept in the photo library: for example the visitor at the door at 17:08. The button is the red circle next to Snapshot (Record Clip).

Prerequisites: an iPhone or iPad, a channel within the plan, and a picture in the view. A few cameras send their picture in a form that cannot be saved as a video; their view has no record button. Apple TV has no record button.

  1. Open the single-camera view of the camera. For a moment in a recording, play the recording from a little before that moment; see “Using the timeline”.

    Check: The picture plays, live or from the chosen time.

  2. Tap the red record button next to Snapshot (Record Clip).

    Check: The button becomes a red stop button (Stop Recording), and a badge with a red dot and the recorded time, for example 0:08, appears at the top of the picture. The clip begins with the next full picture from the camera, which can be a second or two after the tap.

  3. Tap the stop button.

    Check: The sheet Clip slides up. It plays the clip and shows its name, its length and the Share button.

  4. Tap Share.

    Check: The sharing sheet of the system opens. From there, the clip can be sent with a messaging app, saved to the photo library with Save Video, or passed on with the other actions that the system offers. The first time something is saved to the photo library, the system asks whether Cam Hub may add to it.

  5. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The sheet closes, and the view continues.

  • A clip is at most 10 minutes long. At 10 minutes, recording stops by itself, and the sheet says A clip is at most 10 minutes long.
  • Recording also stops by itself when the picture changes: when the timeline is moved, SD or HD is chosen, Back to Live is tapped, or playback continues into the next recording file. The sheet then says Recording stopped because the picture changed. What was recorded up to that moment is kept.
  • When the app leaves the foreground, recording stops and the clip is kept. The sheet appears when the app returns, with Recording stopped when Cam Hub went to the background.
  • Leaving the view while a clip is recording discards the clip.
  • The clip keeps the picture as the camera sent it, in the quality shown (SD or HD), and the sound of the camera when there is one, whether or not the speaker button is on.
  • The file is named after the camera and the moment the clip begins, on the clock of the device, for example Gate 2026-11-02 15.00.04.mp4. The time matches the time printed in the picture.
  • In landscape, the record button is at the top right of the picture, next to the speaker and talk buttons. While a clip records, the stop button stays on screen.
  • When a recording plays as a file (see “While a recording plays”), a share button (Share Recording) takes the place of the record button. It opens the same sheet with the whole recording file.
  • Clips wait on the iPhone for one day after they were made and are then removed; a clip that was sent or saved is not affected. Delete All Data removes them at once.
  • When nothing could be recorded, for example because the picture had not appeared yet, the message Nothing was recorded. Wait for the picture to appear, then try again. appears. When the clip cannot be written, the message is Couldn't save the clip. Check the free space on this device and try again.

Two-way talk

A standalone camera with a speaker can be talked through. Talk works like a walkie-talkie: hold the button to speak, release it to listen. Cam Hub asks the camera for its talk channel when the view opens; the talk button (a microphone symbol) appears to the right of the speaker button when the camera reports one.

Prerequisites: a standalone camera of a supported brand that reports a two-way audio channel, live mode, and a channel within the plan. Channels of a recorder have no talk button: the talk channel of a recorder is the recorder's own audio output, not the speaker of the camera.

The first time the talk button is pressed, a dialog of the system asks for access to the microphone. Allow it, then press the button again.

  1. Press and hold the talk button (Talk) in the controls bar.

    Check: The button shows a spinner while the talk channel of the camera opens, then turns red with a sound-wave symbol. While the button is held, the voice comes out of the speaker of the camera, and the sound of the camera pauses.

  2. Release the button.

    Check: The button shows the microphone symbol again, the microphone stops, and the sound of the camera can be heard again when the speaker is on.

  • Microphone: the microphone is used only while the button is held. After a release, the talk channel of the camera stays open for about 15 seconds, so that the next press talks at once.
  • End of talk: talk ends when the view is left, when the app leaves the foreground and when playback starts. During playback, the talk button is hidden.
  • Landscape: in landscape, the talk button sits at the top right of the picture, next to the speaker button; see “Black bars, rotation and landscape”.
  • VoiceOver: a double tap on the button starts talking, and a second double tap stops it.

Black bars, rotation and landscape

  • Black bars: the picture follows Settings › Picture › Black bars (Default, Enlarge to fill or Stretch to fill; see “The device page”). The single-camera view has no switch of its own; a change in Settings applies to this view as well.
  • Edge-to-edge video: in landscape, the picture extends into the area of the Dynamic Island and the rounded corners when Settings › Picture › Edge-to-edge video is on. When the setting is off, the picture stays clear of them, with black bars.
  • iPad: the layout does not change with the orientation. The view keeps the arrangement of the upright iPhone in both orientations, and the picture-only landscape layout below is not used.

Watching in landscape on an iPhone

Prerequisite: the rotation lock of the iPhone is off.

  1. Turn the iPhone sideways.

    Check: The picture fills the whole screen. The navigation bar, the tab bar, the status bar, the controls bar and the recording panel are hidden. The back button and the other buttons of the picture show for about 5 seconds and then fade out.

  2. Tap the picture once.

    Check: The back button appears at the top left, with the badge next to it. At the top right appear the red record button, the speaker button when the camera sends sound and the talk button when the camera can be talked through. The camera control button is at the lower right when the camera can be moved. The buttons fade out again after about 5 seconds; another tap on the picture shows them again. While a clip records, they stay on screen.

  3. Tap the back button at the top left, or turn the iPhone upright again.

    Check: The view leaves the landscape layout or closes. The back button returns to the page from which the view was opened.

Zoom, pan, PTZ control and clip recording work in landscape as in upright orientation. Snapshot, the SD/HD switch, the day and the list of recordings are available in upright orientation only. For the strip that appears in landscape during playback, see “Playback in landscape”.

PTZ control: moving the camera

Prerequisites: a camera with a movable head (pan, tilt or zoom), a device that supports PTZ control, an account that may control PTZ on the device, and live mode. During playback, the camera control is hidden.

Cam Hub asks the device for the abilities of the channel when the view opens. The round button at the lower right of the picture appears when the answer arrives, which can take a moment. A camera that cannot move shows no button. Only the controls that the device reports are shown.

  1. Tap the round button at the lower right of the picture (Move camera).

    Check: The pad opens to the left of the button, and the button shows a cross (Hide camera controls). The pad holds a capsule with plus and minus for zoom, a disc with four arrows and a house symbol in the middle, and a star button. For a camera that supports aiming by touch, an aim button with a target symbol is added next to the disc, above the star.

  2. Press and hold an arrow of the disc (Tilt up, Tilt down, Pan left or Pan right).

    Check: The camera turns in that direction for as long as the finger stays on the arrow, and the picture follows. The camera stops when the finger is lifted.

  3. Press and hold plus or minus on the capsule (Zoom in or Zoom out).

    Check: The lens of the camera zooms in or out for as long as the finger stays, and stops when the finger is lifted.

  4. Tap the house symbol (Home position).

    Check: The camera moves to its home position.

  5. Tap the star button (Saved positions).

    Check: The star shows a spinner while the positions are read from the camera. Then a dialog lists the names of the positions that are stored in the camera. When the camera has none, the dialog shows This camera has no saved positions. When the positions cannot be read, it shows Could not load the saved positions. Please try again.

  6. Tap the name of a position.

    Check: The camera moves to that position, and the list closes. Tap outside the list to close it without moving the camera.

  7. Tap the cross button (Hide camera controls).

    Check: The pad closes, and a movement that is still running stops.

Aiming by touching the picture

On cameras that support it, the camera can be turned toward a spot of the picture, or zoomed straight into an area drawn on the picture. Prerequisite: the pad is open and shows the aim button.

  1. Tap the aim button (3D positioning).

    Check: The aim button turns white, and the pad shrinks to this button so that it does not cover the picture. The hint Tap to center here. Drag a box to zoom in on it; drag up and to the left to zoom out. appears at the top of the picture.

  2. Tap a spot on the picture.

    Check: A ring marks the spot briefly, and the camera turns so that the spot moves to the middle of the picture.

  3. Drag a box over an area, from its top-left corner to its bottom-right corner.

    Check: A dashed box with a plus magnifier follows the finger. When the finger is lifted, the camera turns to the area and zooms in on it.

  4. Drag a box from the bottom right up to the top left.

    Check: The box shows a minus magnifier. When the finger is lifted, the camera zooms out.

  5. Tap the aim button again.

    Check: The full pad returns, and a drag on the picture moves the enlarged picture again.

  • The touch is measured on the picture itself: black bars, the black-bar mode and the digital enlargement of the view are taken into account. A tap on a black bar sends nothing.
  • When the camera refuses the new position, the hint is replaced for a few seconds by The camera did not take the new position.
  • Closing the pad with the cross button also ends aiming, as does the start of playback.
  • The close-ups on full-screen walls and LiveView Mix walls have the same pad without the aim button.

Notes on PTZ control

  • Speed: the app has no speed setting. A button moves the camera at one fixed speed, the highest that the app requests, for as long as it is held; a short tap makes a short movement.
  • Refused movements: when the device refuses a movement of the pad, no message is shown.
  • Saved positions: Cam Hub recalls positions that exist on the camera. Positions are created and changed on the device itself, not in Cam Hub.
  • Stopping: a movement stops when the finger is lifted, when the view is left, when the app leaves the foreground, when the pad is closed and when playback starts. The same pad is available in the close-up of a camera on full-screen walls and on LiveView Mix walls.

A camera in a window of its own

On an iPad and on the inner display of iPhone Duo, Cam Hub can show several windows. A camera can then be opened in a window of its own, for example to keep the wall in one window and a camera with its recordings and PTZ pad in another, or to place the camera next to another app. On other iPhones, the command is not shown.

  1. On the device page, touch and hold the thumbnail of a camera.

    Check: A menu opens with Open in New Window at the top.

  2. Tap Open in New Window.

    Check: A new window opens with the single-camera view of that camera.

In the single-camera view itself, the Open in New Window button at the right of the navigation bar does the same.

  • Each window is a viewer of its own, with its own timeline and playback. Two windows of the same camera share the live picture of the device.
  • The window is closed with the window controls of the system.
  • When the camera has been deleted, the window shows an empty black page.

The view on a folded iPhone Duo

On iPhone Duo, the single-camera view follows the fold of the inner display. Opened flat, the view looks as on any other iPhone. For the folding positions, see “iPhone Duo”.

Half folded, standing

Prerequisite: the iPhone Duo is held sideways and half folded, so that the fold runs from left to right across the screen, for example when it stands on a table like a small laptop.

  1. Open the single-camera view and fold the iPhone Duo halfway.

    Check: The picture fills the upper half. The controls bar, the recording panel and the PTZ pad are on the lower half, within reach and clear of the fold. The navigation bar, the tab bar and the status bar are hidden; the back button, the badge, the speaker button and the talk button float over the picture as in landscape and fade out after about 5 seconds. A tap on the picture shows them again.

  2. Open the iPhone Duo flat again.

    Check: The usual layout returns.

Held like a book

Prerequisite: a camera of a device that supports playback, and the iPhone Duo held upright and half open, so that the fold runs from top to bottom. For a camera without playback, the usual layout stays.

  1. Open the single-camera view and hold the iPhone Duo half open like a book.

    Check: One half shows the live view as usual, with its controls bar and recording panel. The other half shows the same camera at the same time of day yesterday, on the clock of the device, with a timeline of its own. When nothing was recorded at exactly that time, it starts at the nearest recording. In left-to-right languages the live view is on the left half; in right-to-left languages the halves are swapped.

  2. Drag the timeline of either half.

    Check: That half plays from the chosen time; the other half continues unchanged.

  3. Open the iPhone Duo flat again.

    Check: The usual layout returns.

On the device page held like a book, the camera wall fills one half and the view of the selected camera the other. A tap on a thumbnail shows that camera on the other half instead of opening a new screen, and the selected thumbnail has a colored frame. At first, the first camera that is online, enabled, within the plan and not paused is selected.

YouTube and Twitch streams

YouTube live streams and Twitch channels can be added like cameras; see “Adding YouTube live streams and Twitch channels”. They play in the official player of the service, not through the camera functions of Cam Hub. A stream does not count toward the plan and is never locked; see Chapter 11, “Plans and limits”.

  • On a wall: the tile plays the stream without sound and takes no touches of its own. It has no number and name label in its corner. A tap on the tile opens the stream full screen.
  • Full screen: the picture is as large as the screen allows, on black. The player chooses its quality from the size of the picture, so full screen plays in a higher quality than a tile. The controls of the service work here. A tap on the logo or the title of the service in the player opens the stream in the YouTube or Twitch app or in the web browser.
  • Sound: only in full screen, and only while the speaker switch is on. The speaker button next to Close is the same app-wide switch as the speaker button of a camera (see “Snapshot, sound and quality”): when sound was switched on for a camera, the stream plays with sound as soon as it opens.

  1. On the Live tab or on the device page, tap the tile of a YouTube or Twitch stream.

    Check: The stream opens full screen; when the tile was already playing, the picture continues without loading again. The close button (Close) and the speaker button sit in a strip beside the picture: above it when the iPhone is upright, at its side when it is held sideways. Nothing covers the player.

  2. Tap the speaker button (Unmute).

    Check: The symbol changes to a speaker with sound waves, and the stream plays with sound.

  3. Tap Close.

    Check: The wall is shown again, and the tile plays on without sound.

Connection states and messages

The state of the stream is shown over the picture.

Over the pictureMeaning and action
Spinner with Connecting…The first connection is being made.
Spinner with Reconnecting…The stream was interrupted, or the picture stopped arriving for about 15 seconds while the connection stayed open, as with a frozen camera. Cam Hub tries again by itself at growing intervals of up to about 15 seconds, and about a minute apart after ten failed attempts in a row, until the view is left. An attempt that the device refuses ends at once, without waiting for a timeout.
Message with a warning symbol and RetryThe stream could not be opened. The message names the cause, for example an unreachable device; the wording is the same as when a device is added (see “When adding fails: the checklist”). Tap Retry to try again.

Cam Hub signs in once for all cameras of a device, and after a refusal it does not sign in to the device again for about a minute, so that the account is not locked; Retry within that minute shows the same message.     Check: The message names the cause.

  2. Tap Retry.

    Check: The spinner Connecting… appears. The picture follows, or the message appears again; in that case, work through the checks in “When adding fails: the checklist” and Chapter 15, “Troubleshooting”.

Messages are shown in the language of the app. A few that come from the stream itself can be shown in English whatever the language of the app, for example Connection closed, No video track in SDP or HTTP 404.

A picture that stays black without a message can mean that the device sends a video format that the iPhone cannot decode. Cam Hub plays H.264 and H.265 video only, and a picture size beyond the limit of the decoder of the iPhone is not displayed. A lower resolution or the H.264 format in the settings of the device can help.

When the app moves to the background or the iPhone is locked, the stream stops. When the app returns, the stream connects again by itself. A recording that was playing resumes where it had reached.

What is not available

  • Switching to the next or previous camera by swiping. Each camera is opened from its thumbnail.
  • A button for full screen. Turning the iPhone sideways takes its place.
  • Talk through the channels of a recorder, and talking and listening at the same time: the sound of the camera pauses while the talk button is held.
  • Controls for focus, iris and PTZ speed, and storing or changing saved positions.
  • A snapshot for devices that cannot supply one, and clips longer than 10 minutes; see “Recording a clip”.
