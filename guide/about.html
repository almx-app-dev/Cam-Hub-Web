About this manual

This manual describes Cam Hub 1.8 on iPhone, iPad and iPhone Duo. Apple TV is covered in Chapter 14, “Apple TV”, and the Apple Watch app in “Buttons on Apple Watch”.

Scope

  • App and devices: Cam Hub 1.8 on iPhone, iPad and iPhone Duo. All three run the same app; the manual notes where they differ, and “iPhone Duo” describes the displays of the iPhone Duo. The Apple TV app is not described screen by screen; Chapter 14, “Apple TV” gives the recommended way to bring a setup to it and lists what differs. The Apple Watch app, which sends the requests of control-panel buttons, is described with the control panels in “Buttons on Apple Watch”.
  • Content: every function that the app offers to the user is described: adding devices, YouTube live streams and Twitch channels, live view and talking through a camera, recording playback, LiveView Mix, control panels, disk health, sharing and backup, plans, tools and settings, with the error messages and permission requests that can appear.
  • What the app does not have: Cam Hub sends no notifications, it has no account of its own, and it uses no cloud service for video. Video and sound travel directly between the device and the iPhone or iPad. Only YouTube live streams and Twitch channels come over the internet, played by those services' own players.
  • Brands: only Chapter 16, “Supported brands and connection methods” names brands of cameras and recorders. All other chapters describe functions by capability, for example “devices that support playback”. YouTube and Twitch are video services, not camera brands, and are named where they are described.

Conventions

  • Figures: all figures show the English interface with sample data.
  • Labels: names of screens, buttons and settings are written as the app shows them, in the language of this manual, for example Devices. A path such as Settings › About › Version names the screens in the order in which they are opened. When the app is set to another language, its labels differ; the language is chosen in the Settings tab.
  • Typed values: addresses, port numbers and text to be typed are set in a fixed-width type, for example 192.168.1.64 or 554.
  • Procedures: each numbered step is one action. A line that begins with “Check:” states what the screen shows afterwards. A procedure starts with its prerequisites where there are any.
  • Cross-references: a reference to another chapter or section gives its title, and it is a link.

Where to get help

  • Messages, empty screens, permission requests and common questions are explained in Chapter 15, “Troubleshooting”.
  • Failures while adding a device are covered in “When adding fails: the checklist”.
  • How to reach the developer, and what to include in a message, is described in “Reaching the developer”. The support page of the Cam Hub website, support.html, repeats the notes on compatibility and lists the contact address.
  • In the app, the row User Manual near the end of the Settings tab opens this manual. On an iPhone or iPad, the last row, Contact the Development Team, starts an email to the development team; see “Contacting the development team”.
