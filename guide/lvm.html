8LiveView Mix

LiveView Mix places cameras from different devices on one wall. The wall can also hold pictures, clocks, labels, control panels and YouTube or Twitch streams, and each of them can sit in any tile. This chapter follows the order of the work: the tab and its preview, choosing cameras, layouts, arranging, the optional portrait page, the two canvases of iPhone Duo, the full-screen wall, and the effect of plan limits on the wall.

Terms used in this chapter

Tile: one numbered position on the wall. A tile can hold a camera, a picture, a clock, a label or a control panel, or stay empty. Any tile can be filled; the tiles before it do not have to be filled first.

Layout: the arrangement of the tiles on the screen, for example 2 × 3 or 1 + 5.

Wall page: the tiles that a layout shows at once. When the wall has more tiles than the layout holds, the full-screen wall is divided into wall pages, reached by swiping.

Landscape page and portrait page: two separate walls, one for the device held sideways and an optional one for the device held upright; see “The portrait page”.

The LiveView Mix tab and its preview

Location: the LiveView Mix tab, the leftmost tab at the bottom of the screen. Before setup, the screen shows an explanation and the Set Up LiveView Mix button; for the procedure, see “Building a first wall of six cameras”. Once the wall holds at least one tile with content (a camera, a picture, a control panel, a clock or a label), the tab shows the preview, with the note Preview only — full screen shows your layout. and the Arrangement: Automatic button below it.

Three buttons in the top-right corner are, from left to right:

ButtonPurposeDetails
Full screen (gold wall icon)Opens the wall in the selected layout.“The full-screen wall”
Choose Cameras (camera icon)Selects the cameras on the wall and their quality.“Choosing cameras and quality”
Adjust the Layout (grid icon)Selects the layout, the black-bar handling, the watermark and paging, and opens the arrangement.“Adjusting the layout”

The preview behaves as follows:

  • The preview shows the filled tiles in the order of their numbers, in an even grid. Empty tiles are left out, so a wall with cameras only in tiles 2 and 7 shows two pictures side by side. The preview does not take the shape of the selected layout; the layout takes effect on the full-screen wall only.
  • The preview uses the SD or HD setting of each camera, the black-bar setting of the wall and the watermark.
  • With Automatic (the default), an iPhone held upright shows two columns, and the number of rows depends on the height of the screen. Held sideways, the preview adds columns (typically four) so that two rows fill the screen. On an iPad, the preview normally keeps two columns. When the wall has only one filled tile, that tile fills the whole preview area. A fixed number of columns and rows can be chosen instead; see “The arrangement of the preview”.
  • When there are more tiles than fit, swipe left or right to change the page; page dots appear below the preview.
  • While the watermark is off and the arrangement is Automatic, each camera shows its channel number and name in the bottom-left corner. With a fixed number of columns and rows, the camera pictures carry no label; switch on the watermark to name them. YouTube and Twitch tiles never carry this label. When the watermark is on, the watermark replaces this label.
  • Tapping a camera picture opens the full-screen viewer; see “Opening a single camera”. Tapping a YouTube or Twitch tile opens that stream full screen; see “YouTube and Twitch streams on the wall”. Tapping a button of a control panel sends its request; when the button is set to ask for confirmation first, a confirmation dialog appears; see Chapter 9, “Control panels and webhooks”.
  • A camera that cannot be shown keeps its tile and shows a status text; see “When a tile cannot show a camera”.
  • While the full-screen wall is open, the preview pauses its streams; they resume when the tab is shown again. On an iPhone, the preview also pauses while the Choose Cameras or Adjust the Layout page covers it.
  • When the portrait page is switched on, the tab shows the portrait page while it is taller than wide; see “The portrait page”. On an iPhone Duo with two canvases, the tab shows both canvases instead; see “Two canvases on iPhone Duo”.
  • When no tile has anything to show (for example when every tile points at a camera that has been removed), the screen shows LiveView Mix is empty with a pointer to Settings.
Hold the device sideways

The layouts are drawn for a wide screen. Held sideways, the tiles keep the wide shape of camera pictures; held upright, the layout is flipped about its diagonal (see “Black bars, edge-to-edge video and the angle of the device”), unless a portrait page of its own is switched on (see “The portrait page”). The preview and the full-screen wall rearrange themselves with the angle of the device.

The arrangement of the preview

The Arrangement: Automatic button at the end of the preview note sets how many columns and rows of tiles the preview shows on each of its pages. The choice is one of the app settings, not part of the wall: it does not change the full-screen wall, and it travels with the app settings in a backup or a share code (see “What backups and codes contain”). The chosen columns and rows stay the same whichever way the device is held.

  1. In the LiveView Mix tab, tap Arrangement: Automatic below the preview.

    Check: A small window opens over the preview with the Automatic switch, which is on by default.

  2. Switch off Automatic.

    Check: The window shows Columns and Rows, each with − and +, set to 2 and 2, and the preview changes to a 2 × 2 grid.

  3. Tap − or + beside Columns and Rows until the wanted grid is shown. Each count runs from 1 to 4.

    Check: The preview follows every tap, and every tile fills its share of the screen.

  4. Tap anywhere outside the window.

    Check: The window closes, and the button shows the chosen grid, for example Arrangement: 2 × 4.

To return to the automatic grid, switch Automatic on again. When the last page of a fixed grid is not full, its remaining cells stay empty.

Building a first wall of six cameras

Purpose: place six cameras on one wall and view it full screen in the 1 + 5 layout. Time needed: about 5 minutes.

Prerequisites:

  • The devices have been added and offer at least six channels to choose from; for adding, see Chapter 3, “Adding a device”.
  • All six channels are enabled and within the allowance of the plan. The Free plan allows only 2 enabled channels across the whole app; channels beyond that appear on the wall as Disabled or Not in your plan. For the allowances and for enabling channels, see “How plan limits affect the wall” and Chapter 11, “Plans and limits”.
  • The device is an iPhone or an iPad. For Apple TV, see “Differences on Apple TV”.

  1. Tap the LiveView Mix tab at the bottom of the screen.

    Check: When LiveView Mix has not been set up, the screen shows an explanation and the Set Up LiveView Mix button.

  2. Tap Set Up LiveView Mix.

    Check: The Choose Cameras page opens and lists all channels, grouped by device. The top of the page shows the Landscape page and Portrait page switch with Landscape page selected, and below it 0 cameras selected.

  3. Tap the circle in front of the first of the six channels.

    Check: The circle is ticked, and an SD and HD selector appears on the right of the row, set to SD.

  4. Tick five more channels, in the order in which they should appear on the wall.

    Check: The top of the page shows 6 cameras selected.

  5. Optional: tap HD beside each camera that should show a sharper picture.

    Check: The selector of that camera shows HD. The other cameras are unchanged.

  6. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The page closes and the tab shows a preview of the six cameras.

  7. Tap Adjust the Layout (the grid icon) in the top-right corner.

    Check: The Adjust the Layout page opens. The top of the page shows This layout holds 4 · 6 placed. The default layout holds only 4 tiles per page, so six cameras would use two wall pages.

  8. In the Full-screen layout section, tap 1 + 5.

    Check: The layout is highlighted, and the top of the page shows This layout holds 6 · 6 placed.

  9. Scroll down to the Watermark section and switch on Show watermark.

    Check: The section shows the options for position, content and style. Device name and Camera name are on.

  10. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The page closes. Every camera in the preview is marked with its device name and camera name.

  11. Turn the device sideways.

    Check: The preview rearranges itself for the new angle. No setting is needed.

  12. Tap Full screen (the gold wall icon) in the top-right corner.

    Check: The wall fills the screen: the first camera is in the large tile in the top-left corner (two thirds of the width and height), and the other five are to its right and below it. A ✕ button is in the top-left corner.

  13. Tap the picture of any camera.

    Check: The screen changes to the single-camera view of that camera, playing in HD, with a back button in the top-left corner.

  14. Tap the back button ‹ in the top-left corner.

    Check: The screen shows the full-screen wall again.

  15. Tap ✕ in the top-left corner.

    Check: The wall closes and the preview in the LiveView Mix tab is shown again.

Expected outcome: the LiveView Mix tab shows a preview of six cameras, and the full-screen wall shows them in the 1 + 5 layout, with the first camera in the large tile. To put a different camera in the large tile, see “Arranging the wall and its tiles”. When some tiles show a status text instead of a picture, see “When a tile cannot show a camera” and Chapter 15, “Troubleshooting”.

Choosing cameras and quality

Path: LiveView Mix › Choose Cameras. The page can also be opened from Settings › LiveView Mix, where the camera selection and the layout options are on one page; see “LiveView Mix in Settings”. When the page is opened from the tab, the switch at the top chooses whether the landscape page or the portrait page is being edited; see “The portrait page”.

ItemDescription

Channel rowsOne section per device and one row per channel: a circle, a thumbnail, the channel number and the name. The thumbnail shows a live picture only for channels that the plan covers and that can play. Otherwise it shows the same status text as a tile on the wall: Paused, Disabled, Not in your plan or No signal; see “When a tile cannot show a camera”. The quality rule of LiveView Mix

  • LiveView Mix has its own quality, set for each tile. It is not affected by Single camera and Multiple cameras under Settings › Default quality; those settings apply to the single-camera view and to the ordinary device walls only.
  • Each tile on the wall plays in SD or HD according to its own setting. When tiles are small, HD and SD look almost the same. Using SD for most tiles and HD only for the cameras that need to be seen clearly reduces the load on bandwidth and on the device.
  • A single camera that is opened from the wall or from the preview always starts in HD, whatever the setting of its tile. In the full-screen viewer of the preview, the quality can be switched for the current viewing; the switch is not saved. See “Opening a single camera”.
  • The thumbnails on the Choose Cameras page and on the arrange canvas always use SD.

To choose cameras:

  1. In the LiveView Mix tab, tap Choose Cameras (the camera icon).

    Check: The Choose Cameras page opens. Cameras that are already selected are ticked.

  2. Tap the circle in front of a channel to add it to the wall.

    Check: The circle is ticked, SD and HD appear on the row, and the count at the top increases by 1.

  3. To change the quality of that camera, tap HD or SD.

    Check: The selector shows the chosen quality. Only this camera is affected.

  4. Optional: to change the quality of all selected cameras at once, tap All SD or All HD.

    Check: The selectors of all selected cameras show the same quality.

  5. To remove a camera from the wall, tap the circle of a ticked channel again.

    Check: The circle is empty again, and the count at the top decreases by 1. The tile of that camera becomes empty; no other tile moves.

  6. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The page closes and the preview is updated to the new selection.

Removing a camera leaves its tile empty

After a camera is unticked, its tile is left empty in place. Every other tile, including pictures, clocks, labels and control panels, keeps its number and its position on the wall. The empty tile can be filled again later in Arrange the wall; see “Arranging the wall and its tiles”.

When the page is opened with the button in the top-right corner of the LiveView Mix tab, Cancel in the top-left corner restores the wall to its state before the page was opened. Done keeps the selection.

Tiles that point at removed cameras

When a tile on the wall points at a camera that Cam Hub no longer has (for example when its device no longer reports that channel), the top of the Choose Cameras page shows 2 slots point at cameras that were removed. and the Empty Those Slots button. These tiles do not appear in the list of ticks.

  1. Tap Empty Those Slots.

    Check: The note and the button disappear. These tiles become empty in place, and all other cameras keep their positions.

The top of the page shows This layout holds 6 · 6 placed: the first number is the number of tiles per wall page of the selected layout, the second is the number of tiles on the wall that hold content. Placed tiles include cameras and also configured pictures, control panels, clocks and labels, but not empty tiles.

The Full-screen layout section lists the ready-made layouts in order, with Custom last. Each layout is shown as a small diagram of its shape, with its name below. On the portrait page, the diagrams are drawn tall, because that wall is laid out for an upright screen.

To select a layout:

  1. In the LiveView Mix tab, tap Adjust the Layout (the grid icon).

    Check: The Adjust the Layout page opens. The layout in use is highlighted.

  2. In the Full-screen layout section, tap the layout to use.

    Check: The selected layout is highlighted, and the number of tiles per page at the top is updated.

  3. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The page closes. The preview keeps its even grid, and the selected layout takes effect on the full-screen wall.

The same page also holds the arrangement, the black bars, the watermark and the paging option, described in “Arranging the wall and its tiles”, “Black bars, edge-to-edge video and the angle of the device”, “Watermark” and “LiveView Mix in Settings” respectively.

The scope of Cancel and Done

The Choose Cameras and Adjust the Layout pages that open from the LiveView Mix tab each have Cancel and Done. Cancel restores every wall to its state before the page was opened, including any arrangement, custom layout, work on the other page and the portrait page switch. Custom Layout, Arrange and Slot N each have their own Cancel and Done: Done only returns to the previous level, and Cancel restores only what was changed since that level was entered. To keep all changes, tap Done all the way back to the outermost page.

Drawing a custom layout

Path: LiveView Mix › Adjust the Layout › Draw a custom layout. When none of the ready-made layouts fits, the number of rows and columns of the grid can be chosen freely, and neighboring cells can be merged into larger tiles. On the portrait page, the grid is drawn tall.

  1. In the LiveView Mix tab, tap Adjust the Layout.

    Check: The Adjust the Layout page opens.

  2. In the Full-screen layout section, tap Draw a custom layout.

    Check: The Custom Layout page opens: the grid is at the top, with Rows, Columns and Tiles below it.

  3. Set the size of the grid with − and + on the right of Rows and Columns.

    Check: The grid is redrawn with the new number of rows and columns, and Tiles is updated. When rows or columns are reduced, any large tile that no longer fits is removed.

  4. Drag one finger across the grid, from the first cell to be merged to the last.

    Check: During the drag, the covered area is highlighted. After the finger is lifted, the cells in that area are merged into one large tile, Tiles decreases and the numbers are reassigned.

  5. To split a large tile, tap it once.

    Check: The large tile is split into single cells, and Tiles increases.

  6. Tap Use this layout.

    Check: The button is replaced by Your wall is using this layout.

  7. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The screen returns to the Adjust the Layout page. The Custom diagram is selected and shows the shape that was drawn last.

  • When a drag touches existing large tiles, those tiles are split first, and then only the dragged rectangle is merged. The area is never enlarged automatically.
  • When a drag goes beyond the edge of the grid, the area stops at the cells on the edge.
  • Every change to the grid and the large tiles is stored immediately. Tapping Cancel in the top-left corner restores everything that was changed since the page was opened.
  • Drawing a layout without tapping Use this layout does not change the current wall; a shape that is not in use can also be tidied up here.
  • The drawn layout is kept even when a ready-made layout is selected for a while. Tapping the Custom diagram later returns to that shape.
  • The custom layout can also be selected in the layout row at the top of the Arrange page, but its shape can only be edited here.
  • When the device is held upright, a drawn layout is flipped like the ready-made ones, but each camera stays in the tile it was placed in; see “Black bars, edge-to-edge video and the angle of the device”.

Special layouts: big and small tiles on one wall

A custom layout can merge neighboring cells into large tiles. This makes a layout like the video wall of a monitoring center: the important cameras are enlarged, the others are reduced to small tiles, and clocks, labels or control panels share the same wall.

Example: a grid of 4 rows and 4 columns, with a 2 × 2 large tile merged at the top left and at the bottom left, 10 tiles in all. The 2 large tiles and 6 of the small tiles on the right hold 8 cameras; the remaining 2 small tiles hold a control panel and a clock.

Prerequisites: 8 cameras have been selected for the wall as described in “Choosing cameras and quality”, and the drag operation in “Drawing a custom layout” has been read. Time needed: about 3 minutes.

  1. In the LiveView Mix tab, tap Adjust the Layout, then tap Draw a custom layout.

    Check: The Custom Layout page shows the starting shape: a 3 × 3 grid with a 2 × 2 large tile at the top left.

  2. Set Rows to 4 and Columns to 4.

    Check: The grid becomes 4 × 4, the large tile at the top left is kept, and Tiles shows 13.

  3. Drag one finger across the grid, over the 2 × 2 cells at the bottom left (the third and fourth rows, first and second columns).

    Check: The four cells are merged into a second large tile, and Tiles shows 10. The large tiles are numbered 1 and 6.

  4. Tap Use this layout, then tap Done.

    Check: The screen returns to the Adjust the Layout page, and the Custom diagram is selected.

  5. Tap Arrange the wall and drag the cameras to be enlarged onto the large tiles numbered 1 and 6.

    Check: The dragged camera and the content of the target tile swap places. For the arrangement, see “Arranging the wall and its tiles”.

  6. On the Arrange page, give the two unused tiles 9 and 10 a control panel and a clock: tap each tile, choose Picture, control panel, clock or label…, and set Shows to Control for one and to Clock for the other.

    Check: The canvas shows the control panel and the clock in the two tiles. For the setup, see “What a tile can show besides a camera”.

  7. Tap Done repeatedly to return to the LiveView Mix tab, turn the device sideways, and tap Full screen.

    Check: The wall shows two large tiles and eight small tiles of mixed sizes, as in the picture below.

Expected outcome: the wall follows the layout that was drawn, and the large tiles hold the important cameras. For control panels, see Chapter 9, “Control panels and webhooks”. The tiles, the positions of the large tiles and the content of each tile are stored on this device; when changing devices, a backup and restore can carry them along; see “LiveView Mix in Settings”.

Arranging the wall and its tiles

Path: LiveView Mix › Adjust the Layout › Arrange the wall. This page decides what goes into each tile. Its title is Arrange. From top to bottom, the page holds the layout row, the canvas, Tips and the Order list. Arrange the wall is available as soon as cameras have been selected or a device exists.

  • Layout row: the same layout diagrams as on Adjust the Layout; the layout can be changed here directly. The Custom diagram appears once a layout has been drawn or while it is in use, and can only be selected here.
  • Canvas: drawn in 16:9 landscape for the landscape page and in 9:16 portrait for the portrait page, showing the current wall page. The number of each filled tile is in its top-left corner. Camera tiles show a live picture (SD); a camera that is offline or outside the plan shows a black tile with a crossed-out camera icon, and a YouTube or Twitch stream shows its name. While the watermark is off, the channel name is in the bottom-left corner; when it is on, the watermark is previewed. Black bars, edge-to-edge video and the angle of the device

Path: LiveView Mix › Adjust the Layout › Black bars on this wall. When the shape of a camera picture differs from the shape of its tile, black bars appear. Black bars on this wall decides how all camera pictures on the wall handle them. There are three options:

  • On Apple TV, these tiles show the stream name only; see “Differences on Apple TV”.

When a tile cannot show a camera

A camera that cannot be shown keeps its position on the wall, and its tile shows a dark plate with a status text. Because the position is kept, no other tile moves.

DisplayMeaningWhat to do

DisabledThe channel has not been enabled.Enable the channel on the device page or in Settings › Choose which cameras to show; see Chapter 4, “The Devices tab and channels”.
Not in your planThe channel is enabled but exceeds the allowance of the current plan.See “How plan limits affect the wall” and Chapter 11, “Plans and limits”.

When every tile of the wall, up to the last filled one, shows No signal, Disabled, Not in your plan or Camera removed, the full-screen wall shows These cameras aren't set up here instead of the plates; see “The full-screen wall”.

  • While a tile is connecting, it shows a spinning circle. When the connection fails, the tile explains the reason in a short text; a temporary failure is retried automatically.
  • While Keep cameras connected between pages is off, a tile first shows only the camera name at the moment its wall page comes into view, and the picture follows.
  • Hide offline cameras in Settings applies to the walls of the Live tab only. It does not remove tiles from LiveView Mix.
  • Deleting a device in the app empties the tiles that belong to it, on every wall. No other tile moves, and control panels keep their places too.

How plan limits affect the wall

LiveView Mix itself does not limit the number of cameras on the wall. The number of channels that can show a picture at the same time is decided by the plan; for the details of the allowances, see Chapter 11, “Plans and limits”.

  • Free plan: at most 2 enabled channels across the whole app (single cameras and recorder channels together), and at most 1 recorder. A channel beyond the allowance shows Disabled on the wall; a channel that is enabled but exceeds the allowance shows Not in your plan. In both cases, the position of the tile is kept.
  • Camera plans and recorder plans: these raise the number of single cameras that can be enabled and the number of recorders, respectively. With a recorder plan, all channels of the subscribed recorders can be used.
  • Enabling channels: after a plan is upgraded, channels that were disabled are not enabled automatically; they must be switched on on the device page or in Settings › Choose which cameras to show. A plan with no limit on either axis enables all channels automatically, and Choose which cameras to show is then not shown in Settings.
  • Control panels use the camera allowance, after the cameras: each control panel counts as one single camera against the camera allowance of the plan, and not against the recorder allowance. Panels only use what the cameras leave of that allowance (on the Free plan, what the cameras and recorder channels leave), so a control panel never takes a picture away from a camera. When the allowance is used up, the control panel stays in place, but its buttons are locked. On the full-screen wall, the tile shows a lock, the panel title and Not in your plan (a small tile shows only the lock and the title, or only the lock). In the preview, the tile shows a lock and Not in your plan. The arrange canvas still draws the panel with its buttons. Pictures, clocks and labels do not use the allowance.
  • Each panel on each wall counts: the control panels of the landscape page always count; those of the portrait page count while it is switched on, and those of the lower canvas while the two canvases of an iPhone Duo are switched on. A panel copied to the other page counts on its own. Panels are covered in the order landscape page, lower canvas, portrait page, each wall in tile order.
  • YouTube and Twitch streams: never count toward the plan and are never locked.
  • Locked channels: tapping a camera in the preview that the plan does not cover opens the plans page. On the Choose Cameras page and on the arrange canvas, these channels show no live picture.
  • Custom layouts: at most 8 × 8, which is 64 tiles; see “Drawing a custom layout”.

LiveView Mix in Settings

Path: Settings › LiveView Mix. The LiveView Mix section lists one row per wall, each with the number of selected cameras at its end (6 cameras), or Not set up before setup:

  • LiveView Mix: the wall. While the portrait page is switched on, this row is called Landscape page; on an iPhone Duo with two canvases, Upper canvas.
  • Portrait page: shown while the portrait page is switched on.
  • Lower canvas: shown on an iPhone Duo while the two canvases are switched on.

Tapping a row opens a page that holds the camera selection, the full-screen layout, the watermark and paging of that wall on one page. The options work as described in the sections above. Cancel in the top-left corner restores everything changed since the page was entered, and Done in the top-right corner keeps the changes.

Below the LiveView Mix section, the Portrait page switch has a section of its own (see “The portrait page”), and on an iPhone Duo, so does the Two canvases switch (see “Two canvases on iPhone Duo”). Both are off by default.

Open on launch

Open on launch decides which screen the app shows when it starts:

  • Last viewed (the default).
  • LiveView Mix: opens the LiveView Mix tab.
  • LiveView Mix Full Screen: opens the LiveView Mix tab and goes straight into the full-screen wall after the start, once. Tapping ✕ returns to the preview in the tab.
  • Each device: opens the Live tab and shows that device.

The options LiveView Mix and LiveView Mix Full Screen appear only after the wall has been set up, or while the portrait page or the two canvases are switched on.

  1. Tap the Settings tab at the bottom of the screen and scroll to the LiveView Mix section.

    Check: The screen shows the LiveView Mix row and the Open on launch menu.

  2. Tap Open on launch.

    Check: The screen shows the list of options, with the current option ticked.

  3. Select LiveView Mix Full Screen.

    Check: The menu shows LiveView Mix Full Screen. The next time the app starts, it goes straight into the full-screen wall.

Keep cameras connected between pages

Keep cameras connected between pages in the Paging section is off by default. When it is on, wall pages that are not being looked at stay connected, so the picture appears at once when swiping instead of a black tile while the camera reconnects. The cost is more bandwidth and battery; the effect is largest for recorders that sign in with a session. At most 12 streams are kept. When the switch is turned off, the kept connections are closed at once. The switch applies to the streams of the whole app.

  1. On the Adjust the Layout page, scroll down to the Paging section.

    Check: The screen shows the Keep cameras connected between pages switch with its explanation.

  2. Switch on Keep cameras connected between pages.

    Check: The switch is on.

Backup and restore

The settings of every wall (the landscape page, the portrait page and the lower canvas, each with its cameras and tiles, layout, custom layout, watermark, control panels and pictures), the portrait page and two canvases switches and the arrangement of the preview belong to the app settings and are stored with an iCloud backup (see “Several named backups: renaming and deleting”); when cameras are exported, switching on Include app settings carries them along as well. On restore, a camera tile whose device the backup does not bring back becomes an empty tile; no other tile moves. Pictures make a backup larger, and a picture copied to the other page counts twice. For the procedure, see Chapter 10, “Sharing and backup”.

Differences on Apple TV

Apple TV shows the wall that arrives with a backup or a share code, and it can choose the cameras of that wall itself in Settings › LiveView Mix: select a camera once to add it at SD, again for HD and once more to remove it; All SD and All HD set the quality of every chosen camera at once. The row under Full-screen layout chooses a ready-made layout, or a custom layout that was drawn on an iPhone or iPad, and Black bars on this wall sets the black bars. Arranging the wall, editing its tiles, drawing a custom layout and creating control panels are done on the iPhone or iPad; see “The LiveView Mix tab”. Apple TV shows the landscape page only: the portrait page and the lower canvas of an iPhone Duo are not shown, and their control panels do not count toward the plan there. Control panels appear, and can be pressed, on the full-screen wall only; see “How a panel looks on the wall”. YouTube and Twitch tiles show the stream name with the note that these streams play only on iPhone and iPad.
