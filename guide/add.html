3Adding a device

Adding a camera or recorder requires its address and its account. Cam Hub finds out by itself how to connect to the device; a connection method and ports can be chosen under Advanced instead. YouTube live streams and Twitch channels are added from screens of their own.

The Add Device form

  1. Tap the Devices tab at the bottom of the screen.

    Check: The screen shows the device list.

  2. Tap the ＋ button in the top-right corner. When the list is empty, the Add Device button in the middle of the screen opens the form directly.

    Check: A menu opens that contains Add Device, and, where they are offered, YouTube Live Stream and Twitch Channels.

  3. Select Add Device.

    Check: The screen shows the add-device form with the sections Device and Credentials, and the folded Advanced section.

  4. Optional: enter a name for the device in the Name (optional) field.

    Check: The field shows the name, which is later shown in the device list.

  5. Enter the address of the device in the IP address or hostname field, for example 192.168.1.64.

    Check: The Add button in the top-right corner is available.

  6. Enter the account of the device in the Username and Password fields.

    Check: Both fields are filled in. The password is masked with dots.

  7. Tap Add in the top-right corner.

    Check: The form shows Connecting to device… with the current step below it, for example Looking for open ports… or Trying ONVIF on port 8899…. When the connection succeeds, the form closes and the new device appears in the device list.

While the connection is made, a progress indicator replaces the Add button and the form cannot be closed with a downward swipe. Cancel stops the attempt, and nothing is saved. After connecting, Cam Hub identifies whether the device is a recorder or a single camera, reads the channel list and stores the device.

When the new device is a network storage device (NAS) whose disks Cam Hub can read over SNMP, the SNMP Settings sheet follows. This step is optional: Not Now closes the sheet, and SNMP can be set up later on the Disk Health page; see “Disk Health”. The device is stored either way.

The free plan enables only the channels within its allowance; the remaining channels are shown as Disabled. When the number of recorders allowed by the plan has been reached, the plans page opens. When the demo devices hold the recorder place of the free plan, the page says so; deleting the demo devices frees the place. YouTube live streams and Twitch channels never count toward a plan. For the allowances, see Chapter 11, “Plans and limits”.

The address of a stored device cannot be edited

A stored device can be renamed, and its username and password can be changed with Edit Credentials; see “Changing the user name and password of a device”. Its address cannot be changed. When the IP address changes, the new address is treated as a different device, and a recorder also counts against the recorder allowance. In that case, delete the old device first (see Chapter 4, “The Devices tab and channels”) and add the new address. Adding a device again with the same address, port and username updates the stored device in place instead of creating a second one.

Adding without choosing a brand

With the default Auto-detect, the address and the account are all that Cam Hub needs. The add runs in two stages:

  1. Port check. Cam Hub checks which of the usual ports of cameras and recorders are open at the address: ports of web and control interfaces, ONVIF, RTSP, and the own protocol of a device family. The check sends no username and no password, so it cannot count toward locking the account. When nothing answers, the check is repeated once with longer waits.
  2. Connection attempts. Cam Hub then tries each way of connecting on the ports that answered, in this order: the manufacturer protocols that are built in, the own protocol of a device family, ONVIF, and plain RTSP. For RTSP, it tries the stream paths of a brand that the device revealed, then the paths that most cameras use, then the paths of every brand in the list. The first way that connects is kept, together with the port on which it connected.
  • Ports entered under Advanced are tried first. A port that differs from the default (80 or 554) and is open is used alone, because at an address that forwards several devices another port may belong to another device.
  • To protect the account, the attempt ends at once with Wrong username or password. when a manufacturer protocol refuses the sign-in. ONVIF and RTSP may use an account of their own, so they end the attempt after the second refused sign-in. After a refusal, stream paths are tried one at a time.
  • A camera that is reached only over RTSP is always added as a single camera. To add a recorder that offers only RTSP, select its brand and enter the number of cameras; see “Choosing the connection method”.
  • The own protocol of a device family is tried automatically only at addresses in the local network and on its default port. For such a device outside the local network or on another port, select its connection method under Advanced; see “Connection methods”.
  • Trying many stream paths takes time. For a camera that leaves wrong paths unanswered, the add can take more than a minute before it succeeds or fails.
  • When adding fails, the open ports that were found are listed below the error message, for example Open ports found: 80, 554, or the form shows No open ports were found at this address. The first means that the device answered but would not connect; the second, that nothing answered at all.

For the brands that Auto-detect recognizes and the exact order of the attempts, see “Connection methods”.

Choosing the connection method

Auto-detect suits most devices. When it cannot find the device, or when a particular protocol is wanted, select the connection method under Advanced.

  1. In the add-device form, tap Advanced.

    Check: The section opens and shows Connect using, HTTP Port, RTSP Port and the switch Use HTTPS.

  2. Tap Connect using.

    Check: The Connect using list opens with a search field. At the top are Auto-detect, ONVIF, the own protocol of one device family and RTSP; below them, Brands lists the supported brands in alphabetical order.

  3. Optional: type part of a brand name in the search field.

    Check: The list shows only the matching choices. A brand is also found by other names under which its cameras are sold.

  4. Tap the connection method.

    Check: The form shows the method in the Connect using row, with an explanation below the section. A brand from the list, and RTSP, add a section with Cameras and a path field; the own protocol of a device family replaces the two ports with Device Port. Use HTTPS is offered only with Auto-detect and ONVIF.

  5. When the device does not use the default ports, change the port fields.

    Check: The fields show the ports that the device uses.

The choices:

  • Auto-detect (default): Cam Hub finds the ports and the protocol by itself, as described in “Adding without choosing a brand”.
  • ONVIF: skips the detection and uses only the open ONVIF protocol, on the port in HTTP Port. Use it for a camera that Auto-detect does not find, or to use ONVIF on a device that also supports its own protocol. ONVIF must be turned on at the device; when the device has a separate ONVIF user list, a user must be created there. Whether recording playback and PTZ control are available depends on what the device offers.
  • RTSP: connects over plain RTSP to a camera of any brand and tries the stream paths that most cameras use. When none of them works, enter the path shown in the manual or on the web page of the camera.
  • The own protocol of a device family: connects over that protocol on Device Port, 34567 unless the port was changed or forwarded. Use it for a recorder that Auto-detect does not find, or for one that is reached from outside the local network. For an address outside the local network, the form shows a warning in orange: the protocol does not encrypt the video, and many devices protect the password only weakly. How you reach a device from outside, for example through a VPN, is your own choice. The device family is named in “Connection methods”.
  • A brand from the list: connects directly over RTSP, using the stream paths documented for that brand. Use it when ONVIF is turned off at the device, or to add a recorder that offers only RTSP. The added section has two fields:
    • Cameras: a number from 1 to 64. With 1, the device is added as a single camera; with 2 or more, it is added as a recorder and counts against the recorder allowance. A network scan that learned the number of channels fills it in.
    • The path field: when left blank, Cam Hub tries the documented paths of that brand in turn and keeps the one that the device answers. The field shows the first path that is tried. Fill it in only to specify a particular path.

The brands whose devices have a manufacturer protocol built into Cam Hub are not in the list; Auto-detect finds them, as the note below the list states. Some brands need a dedicated camera or ONVIF account, created in the manufacturer's app or web interface, before RTSP works; the administrator account alone may be refused. For some brands, the paths vary by model or firmware, so the first attempt may not match. The form shows these notes below the path field. For the list of brands and the notes on each, see “The brands in the list”.

RTSP and a brand from the list provide live view only

These connection methods have no recording playback, no PTZ control and no snapshot. SD uses the second stream of the brand where the device serves it; otherwise SD shows the same stream as HD.

Device control uses HTTP unless HTTPS is turned on

Device control (identifying the device, reading the channel list, searching recordings) uses http://address:port, or HTTPS when it is turned on for the device; the device family with its own protocol uses that protocol instead. A device that accepts HTTPS only is added with Use HTTPS; see “Adding a device over HTTPS”.

Failure messages differ for RTSP and a brand from the list

When the device cannot be reached or times out, only Something went wrong. Please try again. is shown. With these connection methods, Not a supported device. means that none of the candidate paths returned a stream. Common causes are a wrong RTSP port or a video stream that is turned off at the device.

When it is unclear whether a device can be used, tap Compatibility reference in the form to see the supported and unsupported devices. The entry is not shown, and neither is Add Demo Cameras, when the form was opened from a scan result that named the brand of the device and the address has not been changed.

Adding a device over HTTPS

A device whose web page uses HTTPS can be added over HTTPS, also when its plain HTTP is switched off. HTTPS is off unless it is turned on, and it is offered only with Auto-detect and ONVIF: the own protocol of a device family, RTSP and the brands from the list have no web interface for it to protect. A device that has already been added is switched to HTTPS on its device page instead; see “HTTPS for a saved device”.

  1. In the add-device form, enter the address, the username and the password, then tap Advanced.

    Check: The section shows the switch Use HTTPS, turned off.

  2. Turn on Use HTTPS.

    Check: The field HTTPS Port appears with 443, and a note below the section explains the option.

  3. When the device uses another HTTPS port, enter it in HTTPS Port.

    Check: The field shows the port. Left at 443, Cam Hub also looks on 5001 and 8443; any other port is used alone.

  4. Tap Add in the top-right corner.

    Check: The form shows Connecting to device…. When the connection succeeds, the form closes and the new device appears in the device list. Its device page shows the row HTTPS with the port in the information card.

  • The note reads Only for a device whose web page uses HTTPS; leave it off if you are not sure. Cam Hub looks for HTTPS on this port (left at 443, also on 5001 and 8443) and trusts the certificate the device presents now. You can check it later under HTTPS on the device's page. It stays visible while the switch is on, also when Advanced is folded.
  • Looking for HTTPS sends no username and no password: Cam Hub only checks which ports present a certificate. When no port does, the form shows This device did not answer over HTTPS just now. When several ports do, they are tried in turn, so that a port forwarded to another device does not end the attempt.
  • The certificate is trusted as the device presents it while it is added. It can be compared with the certificate of the device's own web page afterwards; see “HTTPS for a saved device”.
  • HTTPS encrypts the control of the device. Video fetched over RTSP is not encrypted by it.

Adding YouTube live streams and Twitch channels

A YouTube live stream or a Twitch channel can be added to the device list and shown like a camera. It is played by the service's own player, needs no account and never counts toward a plan. It has no recordings, no PTZ control and no snapshot. Streams play on iPhone and iPad; Apple TV shows them by name only. YouTube and Twitch are not available in some countries and regions, and there these options do not appear.

The first time a screen for YouTube or for Twitch is opened, a notice appears: Before YouTube streams play or Before Twitch channels play. It explains that the service's own player plays the streams, that the service receives the IP address and information about the device and may use cookies and show ads, and that Cam Hub itself collects nothing. It links the service's terms of service and privacy policy and the Cam Hub Privacy Policy. Nothing of the service loads before Agree and Continue is tapped; Cancel closes the screen. The notice appears once for each service.

Adding a YouTube live stream

  1. In the Devices tab, tap the ＋ button in the top-right corner.

    Check: The menu shows Add Device, YouTube Live Stream and Twitch Channels.

  2. Select YouTube Live Stream.

    Check: On first use, the notice Before YouTube streams play appears. Otherwise, the YouTube Live Stream screen opens.

  3. On first use, read the notice and tap Agree and Continue.

    Check: The screen shows the fields YouTube link and Name (optional).

  4. Paste a link into YouTube link: the link of a live stream (in YouTube: Share, then Copy Link), or the link of a channel in the form youtube.com/channel/… to follow that channel.

    Check: YouTube's player shows the stream below the fields, with Checking that this is a live stream… under it. For a live stream, the LIVE badge and the title of the stream appear.

  5. Optional: enter a name in Name (optional).

    Check: The field shows the name. When it is left empty, the stream is named after its title.

  6. Tap Add in the top-right corner.

    Check: The screen closes and the stream appears in the device list.

  • Add becomes available only when the player reports the stream as live. A recorded video, a broadcast that has ended, and a video whose owner does not allow playback in other apps are refused; the player shows the reason and Try Again.
  • For the link of a channel that is not live at the moment, the screen shows You can follow this channel anyway: it plays here whenever it goes live., and Add is available. The tile of a followed channel checks again every minute and starts by itself when the channel goes live.
  • A link to a channel name that starts with @ cannot be used; the screen shows Use the link of the live stream itself: open it on YouTube, tap Share, then Copy Link. A playlist cannot be added either.
  • Adding the same stream again only renames it.

Adding Twitch channels

  1. In the Devices tab, tap the ＋ button and select Twitch Channels.

    Check: On first use, the notice Before Twitch channels play appears. Otherwise, the Twitch Channels screen opens.

  2. On first use, read the notice and tap Agree and Continue.

    Check: The screen shows the field Channel names or links.

  3. Enter one or more channel names or links, separated by spaces, commas or new lines.

    Check: The list below the field shows each channel that will be added. A channel that is already in the device list shows Already followed; an entry that is not a channel shows a line such as Not a Twitch channel: twitch.tv/directory.

  4. Tap Add in the top-right corner.

    Check: The screen closes. Each new channel appears in the device list as a camera named after the channel.

  • Add is available when the list holds at least one channel that is not followed yet.
  • Nothing is checked online. A channel plays in its tile whenever its streamer is live and shows Offline otherwise; a misspelled channel name stays Offline.

A link entered in the Add Device form

When a YouTube or Twitch link is entered in IP address or hostname, the Add button is unavailable and the form offers Add as a YouTube Live Stream or Add as a Twitch Channel. Tapping it opens the screen for that service with the link already entered; after the stream has been added, both screens close. Where the two services are not offered, the link is treated as an ordinary address.

When adding fails: the checklist

When adding fails, the form shows the error message in red. With Auto-detect, the open ports that were found are listed below it. Below that, the form lists Things to check first. The order of the list depends on the kind of failure, with the most likely cause first. Work through the items from top to bottom, then tap Add again.

Error messageChecks (from top to bottom)
Device unreachable. Check the address and your network.
  1. Check the IP address. Look it up on the router or in the manufacturer's own app, or use the scan in the Tools tab.
  2. Connect the phone to the same Wi-Fi network as the device. Guest networks, a separate VLAN, or client isolation on the router block the connection.
  3. Allow Local Network access: Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network › Cam Hub
  4. Turn off any VPN on the phone while adding a device on the home network.
  5. Make sure the device is powered on and its network cable or Wi-Fi is connected.
  6. Check the HTTP and RTSP ports. The usual ones are 80 and 554; some devices use 8000, 8080, 8899 or 37777.
  7. In the device settings, turn on ONVIF and RTSP. When the device has a separate ONVIF user list, create a user there.
Wrong username or password.
  1. Sign in once on the web page of the device itself to check the username and password. They are case-sensitive.
  2. A brand-new device must be activated first, usually by setting an administrator password, before any app can sign in; see “Activating a new device”.
  3. Too many wrong attempts can lock the account for a while, often about 30 minutes. Wait, then try once with the correct password.
  4. Set the clock of the device to the correct time, for example with network time. A clock that is off by minutes makes ONVIF sign-in fail.
  5. In the device settings, turn on ONVIF and RTSP. When the device has a separate ONVIF user list, create a user there.
  6. Try a different Connect using option under Advanced: ONVIF, or the brand of the camera from the list.
Not a supported device.
  1. Try a different Connect using option under Advanced: ONVIF, or the brand of the camera from the list.
  2. In the device settings, turn on ONVIF and RTSP. When the device has a separate ONVIF user list, create a user there.
  3. Check the HTTP and RTSP ports.
  4. Some devices work only through the cloud app of the manufacturer and offer no local connection. Cam Hub cannot connect to those devices.
Too many requests just now. Try again in a minute.
  1. Wait a minute, then try once more.
  2. When the request is still refused, the account may be locked temporarily after too many wrong attempts, often for about 30 minutes. Wait, then try once with the correct password.
This device did not answer over HTTPS just now.
  1. Open the web page of the device in a browser with an address that begins with https://, to check that the device uses HTTPS and on which port.
  2. Enter that port in HTTPS Port. Left at 443, only 443, 5001 and 8443 are checked.
  3. Turn off Use HTTPS to add the device over plain HTTP.
Something went wrong. Please try again. and any other message not listed above
  1. Check the IP address.
  2. Connect the phone to the same Wi-Fi network as the device.
  3. Check the HTTP and RTSP ports.
  4. Sign in once on the web page of the device itself to check the username and password.
  5. Try a different Connect using option under Advanced: ONVIF, or the brand of the camera from the list.
  6. Some devices work only through the cloud app of the manufacturer and offer no local connection. Cam Hub cannot connect to those devices.

When the failure persists after all items have been worked through, the experimental device report can be used; see “Device report (experimental)”.

Device report (experimental)

Experimental feature, no guarantee of support

The device report is an experimental feature. It may change or be removed at any time. Sending a report does not mean that the developer will add support for the device, and there is no guarantee of a reply, a fix or a timeline. Some devices cannot be supported by design, for example because of closed or proprietary protocols, cloud-only operation, licensing or encryption restrictions, or hardware limits. The feature is not a customer-support or urgent-help channel.

Collecting the details signs in to the device with the account and password that were entered. Failed sign-ins are counted, and may be logged, by the device. When the password may be wrong, correct it first as described in “When adding fails: the checklist” instead of trying repeatedly.

The Send details to the developer button, marked Experimental, appears below the checklist only after adding has failed; it is not shown when adding succeeds. The feature runs only when the button is tapped. It checks the device with read-only requests and assembles a text report for review. Cam Hub never uploads the report by itself; the report is sent only if the person chooses to email it from their own mail account.

Prerequisite: the checklist in “When adding fails: the checklist” has been worked through, and the address, username and password in the form are the ones that the device uses.

  1. In the form of the failed attempt, scroll to the checklist and tap Send details to the developer below it.

    Check: On first use, the screen shows the Device Report page with the full notice Before you collect details.

  2. Read the notice and tap I understand — collect details.

    Check: The screen shows Collecting details… and the progress of each check.

  3. Wait until the collection is complete.

    Check: What we found lists the likely causes and what to try first, in plain language. When nothing stands out, the screen shows Nothing obvious stands out.

  4. Under What to include, switch off the items that should not be sent.

    Check: The text in the The report section updates immediately.

  5. Review the full text in the The report section.

    Check: The report is plain text and is always in English. Data that was masked automatically is replaced by a mask marker.

  6. Tap Email to developer.

    Check: The screen opens the mail composer with the report in the message.

  7. Check the message and send it.

    Check: The mail composer closes and the report page is shown again.

  8. Tap Done in the top-right corner.

    Check: The report page closes and the add-device form is shown again.

  • After the notice has been confirmed once, the report page shows only a short reminder at the top, with About this feature to read the full notice again.
  • During the collection, Stop in the top-left corner ends it early. What has been found so far is kept, the screen shows You stopped the collection, so this report is partial. Nothing has been sent. and offers Collect again.
  • What to include has five items: Network, HTTP fingerprint, Protocol probes, RTSP and video and App trace, each marked with a letter. The findings and the report header are always included.
  • Besides Email to developer, the report can be copied with Copy or passed on with Share…. A copy stays on this device only and expires after about 2 minutes.
  • When no mail account is set up, the screen shows Mail isn't set up on this device: the full report is copied to the clipboard, the mail app opens with the start of the report, and the full report must be pasted into the message.
  • The report leaves the device only when it is sent. Nothing is sent unless you choose to send the email. Your password is never included.

Sign-in behavior

During the collection, requests that need a sign-in are sent one at a time until the device has accepted or refused the credentials. After the device has refused them, the report sends no further sign-ins. When the failed attempt that led to the report was itself a refused sign-in (Wrong username or password.), the report does not try to sign in at all, so that the account is not locked by the device. When Use HTTPS was on, the report first looks for the certificate of the device without signing in, and repeats the attempt over HTTPS with that certificate; when the device presents none, the report does not sign in. The collection sends only read-only requests and does not change any setting on the device.

What the report contains and does not contain

  • Contains: the versions of the app and the system, the model identifier of the phone, the language setting, the selected connection method (with the HTTPS port when Use HTTPS was on), the device address (the last part of a private network address is masked with x; for a public address only its class is given), the ports, the error code of the failed attempt, the detected brand and model, open ports, the headers and structure of the answers of the device (masked), the encoding and parameters of the stream, the difference in clock and time zone, and the steps that were run with their results.
  • Does not contain: the password, the username, the name given to the device, a public address or dynamic domain name, the hardware address of the network adapter, email addresses, tokens and other identifying data, pictures and video. Of a long serial number, only the last four characters are kept.
  • Masking is automatic, and each masked item leaves a marker. The report should still be reviewed before it is sent, and any item can be switched off.
Questions in the email

The body of the email contains several questions, among them the brand and model as printed on the label of the device, the firmware version, the kind of device, the difference between what was expected and what happened, whether the manufacturer's own app or a media player shows the device, and an RTSP address that opens in a media player (with the username, password and address removed first). Answering them helps the developer to assess the device.

Saved reports

Each report is saved on this device, and the five most recent are kept. An entry that shows the saved date, for the previous report, appears below the error message; tapping it opens the report again. A saved report shows its content as it was saved and cannot be switched item by item; to choose the content again, collect the details again.

To delete the saved reports:

  1. At the bottom of the report page, tap Delete saved reports.

    Check: The screen shows the dialog Delete all saved reports?, which explains that every device report saved on this device is removed and that a report that was already emailed is not affected.

  2. Tap Delete saved reports in the dialog.

    Check: All saved reports are deleted. The report on the screen is only shown there and is not saved again.

Finding devices with the network scan

Location: the Tools tab at the bottom of the screen.

When the IP address of a device is not known, the Tools tab can scan a network range. The scan probes the six ports 80, 554, 2020, 8000, 34567 and 37777 and lists the addresses that answer. Then it asks the devices it found who they are, over the discovery methods that many cameras and recorders answer without a sign-in. A device that answers this question but was not found by the port probe is added to the list.

  1. Tap the Tools tab at the bottom of the screen.

    Check: In the Subnet section, the This device row shows the current network address and mask, for example 192.168.1.10/24. The address field and Range are filled in with the current network.

  2. Optional: to scan a different network, enter the network in the address field, for example 192.168.1.0, and select the range in the Range menu.

    Check: Below the field, a line shows the network and the number of hosts that will be scanned, for example Will scan 192.168.1.0/24 · 254 hosts.

  3. Tap Scan.

    Check: The screen shows the progress Scanned 120 of 254, and the button changes to Stop.

  4. Wait until the scan ends; to end it early, tap Stop.

    Check: Found Devices lists the addresses that answered, with the open ports of each address as badges in the row. While the devices are asked who they are, Identifying devices… is shown; then each row that a device answered for shows its name or model, its brand and model, the number of channels of a recorder, and Not activated for a device that still has to be activated. When nothing was found, the screen shows No devices found yet.

  5. Tap a row in the results.

    Check: The screen opens the add-device form with the address already entered in the IP address or hostname field, and with whatever the device reported about itself; continue with the steps in “The Add Device form”.

  • Besides the address, the form takes over what the device reported: its name or model as the name, the port of its management service as HTTP Port, and the number of channels as Cameras for a brand from the list. For a device of the family with its own protocol whose port is not the default, that connection method and its Device Port are selected and Advanced is opened.
  • When the scan reported that the device is not activated, the form starts with a notice that it must be activated first; see “Activating a new device”.
  • Range runs from /26 (smallest) to /16 (largest). When a range holds more than 1,024 addresses, the scan samples them evenly instead of probing each one.
  • When the address field or the range differs from the current network, Use my subnet appears; tapping it restores the current network.
  • When the address field has an invalid format, the screen shows Enter a subnet like 192.168.1 or 192.168.1.0.
  • After the device has been added, the screen returns to the Tools tab, not to the device list.
Limits of the scan results

The scan finds only devices that answer on camera ports or to the discovery request. A device that does not identify itself can be a camera or a recorder; the type is confirmed when the device is added. Its open ports and any server name give a hint about the manufacturer.

When a scan has finished completely without any result, the screen shows If your cameras are on this network, check that Cam Hub is allowed to find devices on your local network in the system privacy settings. When the Local Network permission is denied, the scan shows no error; its result is empty as well. Check that the permission is turned on: Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network › Cam Hub.
