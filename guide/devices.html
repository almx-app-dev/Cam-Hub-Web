4The Devices tab and channels

The Devices tab lists every recorder and camera that has been added, together with YouTube and Twitch streams added as cameras. Opening a device shows its channels as live thumbnails. From there, channels are renamed, disabled, paused or shown in a full-screen wall, the user name and password of the device are changed, and the disks of supported devices are checked. The limits of the plan decide which channels can show a picture.

The device list

Path: Devices tab at the bottom of the screen.

Each row shows the name of the device, its model and its address. The icon on the left marks a recorder or a camera. On the right, a connection indicator sits above a label that names the type: Recorder or Camera. The indicator reports whether the device accepts a connection on its HTTP port, or on its HTTPS port while HTTPS is on for the device (see “HTTPS for a saved device”). The check starts every time the row appears on the screen and waits for an answer for about 4 seconds.

IndicatorMeaning
Small spinning circleThe check is running.
Green Wi-Fi symbolThe device answered (Reachable).
Red, crossed-out Wi-Fi symbolThe device did not answer (Can't reach this device — check the network or firewall). The device may be switched off, on a different network, or blocked by a firewall.
Red play symbol or purple TV symbolA YouTube stream or a Twitch channel. These have no device to test; see “YouTube and Twitch streams”.
The indicator tests one port only

A device that does not accept connections on the tested port can show the red symbol while its video works, for example a camera that was added through a brand from the list. The sample cameras use the address demo, which is not a device on the network, and always show the red symbol.

The plus button at the top right opens a menu. Add Device adds a recorder or a camera; see Chapter 3, “Adding a device”. Where the services are offered, the menu also holds YouTube Live Stream and Twitch Channels, which add a live stream as a camera; see “Adding YouTube live streams and Twitch channels”.

Opening a device

  1. Tap the Devices tab at the bottom of the screen.

    Check: The screen shows the device list. When no device has been added, the screen shows No Devices, the text Add your first camera or recorder to get started. and the Add Device button; see Chapter 3, “Adding a device”.

  2. Tap a row in the list.

    Check: The device page opens; see “The device page”.

Changing the order of the devices

  1. Tap Edit in the top-left corner. The button is shown only while at least one device exists.

    Check: A drag handle appears on the right of every row, and the button now reads Done.

  2. Touch the handle of a row and drag the row to its new position.

    Check: The row stays at the new position.

  3. Tap Done.

    Check: The list leaves edit mode. The order is stored. The device selection list in the Live tab uses the same order.

Deleting a device

Deleting removes the device from Cam Hub. The device itself is not changed.

  1. Swipe left on the row of the device.

    Check: A Delete button with a trash symbol appears on the right of the row. Even a full swipe only opens the confirmation; nothing is deleted without it.

  2. Tap Delete.

    Check: The dialog Delete this device? appears. It names the device and the number of its cameras and states that the action cannot be undone. When a password is saved for the device, it also states that the password is removed.

  3. Tap Delete in the dialog. To keep the device, tap anywhere outside the dialog instead.

    Check: The row disappears. The device, its channels, its saved password, its SNMP settings, its disk readings and any recordings downloaded from it as files (see “While a recording plays”) are removed from this iPhone or iPad. The device is also removed from the LiveView Mix wall, and from the choices of the Live tab and of Open on launch when it was selected there.

A deleted recorder no longer counts against the number of recorders that the plan allows; see “Plan limits and enabling channels”. To add the device again, see Chapter 3, “Adding a device”.

The device page

The device page shows an information card at the top, the Disk Health row for devices that support it, and, under the heading Cameras, the channels of the device as thumbnails. The title is the name of the device. The thumbnails are arranged in as many columns as fit on the screen.

  • Information card: shows the name and the type of the device and the rows Model, IP address, Port (the HTTP port), HTTPS (the HTTPS port, only while HTTPS is on), Serial number, Firmware and Cameras (the number of channels). The serial number and the firmware appear only when the device reports them. For a YouTube or Twitch stream, the row Link replaces IP address and Port.
  • Disk Health: opens the disk report of the device; see “Disk Health”. An orange triangle or a red symbol on the right of the row repeats the worst disk verdict of the last reading.
  • Thumbnails: a recorder shows one thumbnail per channel; a standalone camera has one channel and shows one thumbnail. The lower-left corner of every thumbnail shows the number and the name of the channel. The thumbnails use SD or HD according to Settings › Default quality › Multiple cameras (SD unless changed). A change in Settings applies to this page at once; the page has no SD/HD button of its own.
  • Refresh: Cam Hub reads the channel list from the device when the page opens, at most once a minute for the same device, and every time the page is pulled down. Names and online states are updated, channels that the device has added appear, and channels that the device has removed disappear. A new channel is enabled only while the plan has a free place, and channels with a signal get the free places first (see “Plan limits and enabling channels”); a new channel without a place is shown as Disabled. Channels that were already listed stay enabled or disabled as they were, and names that were set in Cam Hub are kept. When the device does not answer, or returns a list that looks incomplete (no channel at all, or a single channel in place of several), the list stays as it was and no message is shown.
  • Refused sign-in: when the device refuses the saved user name or password during the refresh, the message Wrong username or password. and the button Edit Credentials appear above the thumbnails; see “Changing the user name and password of a device”.
  • No saved password: when no password is saved for the device on this iPhone or iPad, a message with the buttons Enter Password and Restore from iCloud appears above the thumbnails; see “A device without a saved password”.
  • No channels: when the device reports no channel, the page shows No cameras found. and the button Refresh, which reads the channel list again.

The three buttons at the top right are, from left to right: the black-bar button, Full Screen Wall and the pencil. For a device that signs in with a user name and password, the pencil (Edit Device) opens a menu with Rename and Edit Credentials, and with HTTPS when the device serves HTTPS or has it turned on; see “HTTPS for a saved device”. For the sample cameras and for YouTube and Twitch streams, the pencil is the Rename button itself.

iPhone Duo held like a book

When an iPhone Duo is half open with the fold running from top to bottom, the device page shows the thumbnails on one half and the page of one camera on the other. A tap on a thumbnail shows that camera on the other half instead of opening a new page; at first, the first camera that can show a picture is shown. See “The view on a folded iPhone Duo”.

Hiding or showing the details

  1. Tap the arrow on the right of the information card (Hide details).

    Check: The rows below the name disappear, and the arrow changes direction (Show details). Tapping it again shows the rows. The choice is stored for each device.

Renaming a device

  1. Tap the pencil button at the top right of the device page.

    Check: For a device that signs in with a user name and password, a menu with Rename, Edit Credentials and, where offered, HTTPS opens. For other devices, the dialog of the next step opens at once.

  2. When the menu is shown, tap Rename.

    Check: The dialog Rename Device opens, with the text Give this device a name you'll recognize. and the current name in the Name field.

  3. Enter the new name in the Name field.

    Check: The field shows the new name.

  4. Tap Save.

    Check: The title of the page and the device list show the new name. An empty name is ignored and the old name stays. The name is stored in Cam Hub only; the device is not changed.

Changing the handling of black bars on this page

The black-bar button is the left one of the three buttons. Each tap selects the next of three modes; after the third mode the first one returns.

ModeEffect
DefaultThe whole picture is shown. Black bars appear where the shape of the picture differs from the shape of the thumbnail.
Enlarge to fillThe picture is enlarged in proportion until it fills the thumbnail. The edges of the picture are cut off.
Stretch to fillThe picture is stretched until it fills the thumbnail. The picture is distorted.

  1. Tap the left button of the three buttons at the top right.

    Check: All thumbnails change to the next mode, and the icon of the button changes to match the current mode.

The first mode comes from Settings › Picture › Black bars. A change made on this page applies to this page only and is not stored.

Changing the user name and password of a device

When the password of a device is changed on the device itself, for example by an installer or after a reset, Cam Hub can no longer sign in, and the thumbnails show Wrong username or password. Edit Credentials stores the new user name and password for the same device. The cameras, their names, the enabled and paused choices and the places on LiveView Mix walls stay as they are; deleting the device and adding it again would lose them.

Edit Credentials is offered for every device that signs in with a user name and password. It is not offered for the sample cameras or for YouTube and Twitch streams. The address and the ports of the device are not changed here.

  1. Open the device page of the device.

    Check: The device page is shown. When the last refresh was refused, the message Wrong username or password. and the button Edit Credentials appear above the thumbnails; that button opens the same screen as step 3.

  2. Tap the pencil button (Edit Device) at the top right.

    Check: A menu with Rename and Edit Credentials opens; HTTPS follows for a device that offers it.

  3. Tap Edit Credentials.

    Check: The screen Edit Credentials opens. Its section is headed with the name of the device and holds Username, filled in with the current user name, and an empty Password field, where the cursor is. The note below the fields reads The new credentials are checked on the device before they are saved. Its cameras, their names and your LiveView Mix walls stay as they are.

  4. When the user name has changed as well, correct it in Username.

    Check: The field shows the user name. Save is unavailable while Username is empty.

  5. Enter the new password in Password.

    Check: The field shows one dot per character. An empty password is accepted, as when a device is added.

  6. Tap Save.

    Check: A spinner replaces Save while Cam Hub signs in to the device once with the new credentials and reads its channel list. When the device accepts them, the screen closes, the thumbnails connect again with the new credentials, and the channel list is refreshed. When it does not, the screen stays open, nothing is saved, and the reason appears in red, for example Wrong username or password. or Device unreachable. Check the address and your network.

Tap Cancel to close the screen without saving; a check that is still running is stopped and nothing is changed. The new user name must not be one that another saved device at the same address and port already uses; in that case the screen shows Another device at this address already uses that username.

One sign-in per device

Many recorders and NAS units lock the account, or the address of the iPhone or iPad, after a few failed sign-ins, and the lock also affects other apps. Cam Hub therefore checks new credentials with a single sign-in through the connection the device was added with, instead of trying several connection methods. For most devices, the thumbnails of a wall also sign in once for the whole device: when the device refuses, every thumbnail of that device shows the failure at once, and Cam Hub tries the same credentials again only after about a minute or when Cam Hub returns to the foreground. Saving new credentials starts over at once.

A device without a saved password

Cam Hub stores the password of each device in the Keychain of the iPhone or iPad. When the contents of the iPhone or iPad move to a new one, the passwords can move with them; an iCloud backup or a sharing code of Cam Hub carries every password that is saved on the iPhone or iPad where it is made (see “What backups and codes contain”). A device list that arrives without its passwords, for example on a new iPhone, iPad or Mac that was set up without the Keychain of the old one, shows the state below. Cam Hub then sends nothing to the device: it does not sign in with an empty password, and it does not report a wrong password.

  1. Open the device page of the device.

    Check: Above the thumbnails, the page shows No password is saved for this device on this iPhone, iPad or Mac. This can happen after moving to a new one. Enter it again, or restore your iCloud backup. with the buttons Enter Password and Restore from iCloud. The thumbnails show No password saved. Enter it again under Devices.

  2. Tap Enter Password to type the password of this device, or Restore from iCloud to bring back the passwords of every device from an iCloud backup.

    Check: Enter Password opens the screen Edit Credentials, described in “Changing the user name and password of a device”. Restore from iCloud opens the page described in “Restoring from iCloud”.

Once the password is saved or restored, the message disappears and the thumbnails connect again. A restore brings back the passwords of all devices at once; without a backup, they are entered device by device.

HTTPS for a saved device

A device that serves HTTPS on its web interface can be controlled over HTTPS instead of plain HTTP. HTTPS is off unless it is turned on for the device. With HTTPS, Cam Hub accepts only the certificate that was trusted when HTTPS was turned on, or a certificate that a public certificate authority issued for the address of the device, so that automatic renewals of such a certificate keep working. Video fetched over RTSP is not encrypted by this setting.

The pencil menu offers HTTPS for a device that answered over HTTPS, and for a device that has HTTPS turned on. Cam Hub asks the device without signing in: it only checks whether a port presents a certificate. A device that answered is remembered for a week, one that did not for a day; pulling the device page down asks again at once. On Apple TV, which has no pull-down, a device that did not answer is asked again when its device page is opened more than an hour later. The item is not offered for devices connected through the own protocol of a device family, through RTSP or through a brand from the list, nor for the sample cameras and YouTube and Twitch streams.

Turning HTTPS on

  1. On the device page, tap the pencil button (Edit Device) at the top right, then tap HTTPS.

    Check: The screen HTTPS opens with the name of the device as the heading of its section. While Cam Hub asks the device, it shows Checking HTTPS…; then it shows Port, the port on which the device answered, and Certificate (SHA-256), the fingerprint of the certificate that the device presents now, in four lines.

  2. Where possible, compare the fingerprint with the certificate of the device's own web page, for example in the certificate details of a browser.

    Check: The fingerprints are identical. When they differ, another device may be in between; leave HTTPS off.

  3. Turn on Use HTTPS, then tap Save.

    Check: A spinner replaces Save while Cam Hub signs in to the device over HTTPS with the saved credentials and reads its channel list. When this succeeds, the screen closes, the thumbnails connect again, and the information card shows the row HTTPS with the port. When it does not, the screen stays open, nothing is saved, and the reason appears in red.

  • When the device does not answer over HTTPS, the screen shows This device did not answer over HTTPS just now. in orange, and the switch cannot be turned on.
  • The note below the section reads Off unless you turn it on. With HTTPS, Cam Hub talks to this device encrypted and accepts only the certificate shown here, or a certificate that a public certificate authority issued for this address, so automatic renewals keep working; if you can, compare it with the one on the device's own web page. Video sent over RTSP is not encrypted by this setting.
  • Turning HTTPS off: turn off Use HTTPS and tap Save. Cam Hub first checks that the device answers over plain HTTP with the saved credentials. When it does not, nothing is changed and the screen shows This device did not answer over plain HTTP, so HTTPS stays on. Turning HTTPS off forgets the trusted certificate, so turning it on again checks the certificate afresh.
  • Changed certificate: when the device presents a certificate other than the trusted one, and no public certificate authority issued it for the address, Cam Hub does not connect, and the thumbnails show This device's security certificate has changed. Check it under HTTPS in the device's edit menu. The HTTPS screen then shows the new fingerprint with The device now presents a different certificate from the one trusted when HTTPS was turned on. Save to trust it only if you know why it changed, for example after a reset. Save trusts the new certificate after the same check as when HTTPS is turned on.
  • When the device answers on another HTTPS port with the same certificate, the screen shows the new port, and Save stores it without a warning.
  • The HTTPS setting, with its port and trusted certificate, travels with iCloud backups and sharing codes, but a restore or an import never turns HTTPS off; see “What backups and codes contain”.

Disk Health

Disk Health reports the state of the disks of a recorder or a NAS: whether each disk is healthy, its temperature and the drive's own S.M.A.R.T. data, so that a failing disk can be replaced before recordings are lost. Cam Hub only reads this information; nothing on the device is changed.

The row Disk Health appears on the device page of devices that can report their disks: devices added with a brand connection that supports it (see Chapter 16, “Supported brands and connection methods”), and NAS units that share their disk health over SNMP. Devices added through ONVIF or with a stream address, YouTube and Twitch streams, and the sample cameras do not show the row.

Opening Disk Health

  1. Open the device page of the recorder or NAS.

    Check: The row Disk Health appears below the information card. An orange triangle on the right means that a disk needed attention at the last reading; a red symbol means that a disk was failing.

  2. Tap Disk Health.

    Check: The page Disk Health opens and reads the device. For a NAS without SNMP settings, the page shows This NAS shares its disk health over SNMP. Set it up once to see S.M.A.R.T. data, temperatures and the state of its volumes. and the button Set Up SNMP; see "Setting up SNMP on a NAS" below. When the device cannot be read, the page shows the reason and Try Again, and for a NAS also SNMP Settings.

  3. Pull the page down to read the device again.

    Check: The page shows the new reading. When the new reading fails, the previous one stays on the page, with the reason above it and the note Showing the last reading. Pull down to try again.

What the page shows

SectionContent
One section per disk, headed with its name and typeThe verdict (see the next table); Status, the state that the device reports for the disk, in the language of Cam Hub (for example Normal, Sleeping, Not installed or From another recorder; a word that Cam Hub does not know is shown as the device sends it); Capacity; Temperature; Powered on in days and years; one sentence per finding; counters that have risen since Cam Hub first read the disk; and S.M.A.R.T. details, which lists every attribute with its current value, its worst value and its limit (value · worst · limit), in red when it has failed. When a device does not report S.M.A.R.T. data for a disk, the section says so and shows the state of the disk only.
Volumes (NAS)Each volume or disk array with its state (for example Normal, Degraded or Crashed) and its free space.
DeviceSystem temperature (NAS), Uptime, failed parts that the device reports (power supply, fan), and the last restarts that Cam Hub noticed (The device restarted around Oct 14, 3:12 AM.), in orange when they happened within the last week.
Performance (NAS)CPU, Load (1 min) and Memory (used of total, with a bar).
Network (NAS)Each network port with its state and speed, the incoming and outgoing traffic, a warning when a connected port runs below 1000 Mbps or has counted 100 errors or more, and a chart of the traffic over the last few minutes.
Disk activity (NAS)Reading, writing and how busy each disk is, with a chart of the writing over the last few minutes.

While the page of a NAS is open, the sections Performance, Network and Disk activity are read again every 5 seconds; the speeds are averaged over about the last half minute.

Verdict of a diskMeaning
Healthy (green)Nothing to do. A drive powered on for 5 years or more is noted with a hint to plan a replacement.
Needs attention (orange)The drive has not failed, but something is worth acting on: an attribute within a few points of its limit, sectors that were remapped, wait to be remapped or cannot be read, data errors between the disk and the device, retries to spin up, a temperature of 55 °C (131 °F) or more, or a disk state other than normal, for example not formatted.
Failing (red)The device or the drive reports a failure: a disk state such as failed or error, a failed self-assessment or self-test, or an attribute at its limit.

The symbol on the Disk Health row of the device page shows the worst disk verdict of the last reading. The state of volumes and failed parts are shown on the page itself, in the sections Volumes and Device.

Temperatures are shown in the unit set for the iPhone or iPad in the iOS Settings app under General › Language & Region: Celsius or Fahrenheit, following the region unless changed there. The warning about a hot drive uses the same unit, for example Running warm at 133°F. Years above 131°F shorten a drive's life; check ventilation.

Setting up SNMP on a NAS

A NAS reports its disks over SNMP, a read-only service that answers anyone who knows its community name. First switch on SNMP in the management interface of the NAS: turn on the SNMP service, choose SNMPv1/v2c and set a community name. The iPhone or iPad must be able to reach the NAS on the SNMP port, usually in the same local network. A NAS set to SNMPv3 only is not supported. The note on the SNMP Settings screen names the menu of the NAS where SNMP is switched on.

  1. On the Disk Health page of the NAS, tap Set Up SNMP. Once settings exist, tap SNMP Settings (the gear at the top right) instead.

    Check: The screen SNMP Settings opens, with the fields Community (public on the first visit) and Port (161), and a note on where to switch SNMP on.

  2. Enter the community name that is set on the NAS in Community.

    Check: The field shows the community name.

  3. Change Port only when the NAS answers SNMP on a port other than 161.

    Check: The field shows the port.

  4. Tap Save.

    Check: The screen closes, and Disk Health reads the NAS. When a field is empty or the port is not between 1 and 65535, the screen stays open with Enter a community name and a port from 1 to 65535. When the NAS does not answer, the page says so, names the port it asked on, and asks to check that SNMP is switched on and that the community name matches; a blocked port has the same effect. A NAS does not answer a community name it does not know, so a wrong name looks the same as a NAS that cannot be reached.

When a NAS is added with Auto-detect and has no SNMP settings yet, the same screen opens right after the device has been added, as an optional step: Save stores the settings, and Not Now closes the screen. The device is added either way, and SNMP can be set up later from Disk Health; see Chapter 3, “Adding a device”.

The SNMP settings are stored for each device in the Keychain of the iPhone or iPad and are deleted with the device. They are included in share codes and iCloud backups, so Disk Health works after an import or a restore without being set up again; see Chapter 10, “Sharing and backup”.

YouTube and Twitch streams

A YouTube live stream or a Twitch channel that was added as a camera (see “Adding YouTube live streams and Twitch channels”) appears in the device list as a camera with one channel. Its row shows the name, the model (YouTube Live or Twitch) and the short link, for example youtu.be/… or twitch.tv/…. In place of the connection indicator, the row shows a red play symbol for YouTube or a purple TV symbol for Twitch.

  • Device page: the information card shows Model, Link (the short link) and Cameras. There is no Disk Health row and no Edit Credentials; the pencil renames the stream.
  • Thumbnail: the service's own player shows the stream, without sound and without a name label over it. A tap opens the stream full screen; see “YouTube and Twitch streams”. The long-press menu offers Rename Camera and Disable or Enable.
  • Plan: these streams never count toward the plan and never show Not in your plan. Their channel can always be enabled.
  • Terms not yet agreed: a stream that reached this iPhone or iPad without the add form, for example through a share code or a backup, shows only its name and Agree to YouTube's terms to play this stream. or Agree to Twitch's terms to play this channel. A tap opens the full-screen view, where Review Terms… shows the terms with Agree and Continue. Once agreed, every stream of that service plays.
  • Not available: YouTube and Twitch are not available in some countries and regions. There, a stream on the list shows only its name and Not available in this region or language.
  • Deleting: swipe left on the row and confirm, as for any device; see “The device list”. The dialog does not mention a password, because none is saved.

What a thumbnail shows

The content of a thumbnail depends on the state of the channel. The table lists the states in the order in which Cam Hub decides between them. A tap opens the single-camera view only for an online channel that is enabled, within the plan and not paused; see Chapter 6, “Single-camera view”. A tap on a disabled or paused thumbnail offers to switch it back on. YouTube and Twitch thumbnails are described in “YouTube and Twitch streams”.

Hide offline cameras affects the walls only

The setting Hide offline cameras removes cameras without a signal from the live walls. The device page always lists every channel and shows the ones without a signal in grey. LiveView Mix walls are not affected by the setting.

Renaming a channel

  1. Long press the thumbnail of the channel.

    Check: The menu of the channel opens.

  2. Tap Rename Camera.

    Check: The dialog Rename Camera opens, with the text Name this camera yourself. The device's own name is kept underneath, so refreshing the device won't overwrite yours., the current name in the Name field and the buttons Use Device Name, Save and Cancel.

  3. Enter the new name in the Name field.

    Check: The field shows the new name.

  4. Tap Save.

    Check: The lower-left corner of the thumbnail shows the new name. The same name is used on the Live tab, on LiveView Mix walls and as the title of the single-camera view. An empty name, or the name that the device reports, returns the thumbnail to the name of the device.

  5. To return to the name that the device reports, repeat the first two steps and tap Use Device Name.

    Check: The thumbnail shows the name of the device again.

A name set in Cam Hub is not overwritten when the channel list is refreshed, and it is included in backups; see Chapter 10, “Sharing and backup”.

Pausing and resuming the video of a recorder channel

  1. Long press the thumbnail of a channel of a recorder.

    Check: The menu shows the section Bandwidth.

  2. Tap Pause Video.

    Check: The thumbnail turns grey and shows Paused. Every screen that shows this channel treats it as paused.

  3. To resume, tap the paused thumbnail and then Resume Video.

    Check: The live picture returns. Resume Video is also in the long-press menu.

Plan limits and enabling channels

A channel is either enabled or disabled. An enabled channel uses one place of the plan. Only enabled channels within the limits of the plan show a picture; an enabled channel beyond the limits shows Not in your plan. The limits are listed below; the plans themselves are described in Chapter 11, “Plans and limits”.

PlanStandalone camerasChannels of recordersRecorders that can be added
FreeAt most 2 enabled channels in total, counting cameras and recorder channels together. Enabled recorder channels must all belong to the same recorder.1
Camera planEnabled cameras up to the limit of the plan: 8, 16 or unlimited.As on the free plan: 2 enabled channels, all on one recorder.1
NVR planAs on the free plan: 2 enabled cameras.Every channel of the recorders that the plan covers.2, 4 or unlimited
  • Both plans can be held together. The two limits apply independently: the camera plan raises the limit for standalone cameras, and the NVR plan raises the number of recorders whose channels are all usable.
  • Each control panel on a LiveView Mix page that is shown counts as one camera against the camera limit. Real cameras keep their picture first; panels beyond the limit show Not in your plan. See Chapter 8, “LiveView Mix”.
  • A recorder counts toward the number of recorders as soon as it has been added, whether or not any of its channels is enabled. The sample cameras count like real devices.
  • When a device is added, and whenever its channel list is read again and contains new channels, the free places of the plan go to the new channels in this order: first the channels that the device reports with a signal, then the channels without a signal, each group in the order in which the device lists them. New channels that get no place are shown as Disabled. Channels that were already listed stay enabled or disabled as they were. Example: on the Free plan, with no other channel enabled, a recorder whose channels 1 and 2 have no signal and whose channels 3 and 4 have a signal starts with channels 3 and 4 enabled; channels 1 and 2 are shown as Disabled.
  • YouTube and Twitch streams are not counted and are never locked; see “YouTube and Twitch streams”.
  • When a plan ends or its limits fall below the number of enabled channels, the stored choices are kept. The channels beyond the limits show Not in your plan: standalone cameras keep their picture first, then recorder channels, each in the order in which the devices were added and in the order of the channel numbers.
  • When both limits are unlimited, every channel is enabled automatically, and the page Choose which cameras to show is not offered.

Prerequisite: the thumbnail of the channel shows Disabled.

    Check: The device page is shown again.

    Check: The thumbnail shows the live picture.

Choosing which cameras show

Path: Settings › Choose which cameras to show

This page lists the channels of all devices in one place, so that the places of the plan can be assigned across devices without opening each device page. The item is offered only while at least one of the two limits of the plan applies.

  1. Tap the Settings tab at the bottom of the screen.

    Check: The screen shows the settings.

  2. In the Subscription section, tap Choose which cameras to show.

    Check: The page Choose which cameras to show opens. The section Your plan shows the rows Cameras (for example 2 cameras or Unlimited) and Recorders, and the note Turn on the cameras you want to watch. The Free plan shows 2 cameras on a single recorder. Below it, one section per device lists every channel with its number, its name and a switch.

  3. Turn off the switch of a channel that is not needed.

    Check: The switch is off. The channel is disabled and its place in the plan is free. Turning a switch off is always possible.

  4. Turn on the switch of the channel that should be shown.

    Check: The switch is on when the plan has a free place. When it has not, the plans screen opens with a message and the switch stays off. On the free plan, the message On the Free plan, the cameras you show must all be on one recorder. appears when a channel of a second recorder is turned on.

  5. Tap the back button in the top-left corner to leave the page.

    Check: The choices apply everywhere: walls and the device pages show a picture for the enabled channels within the plan only.

When no device has been added, the page shows No Devices, Add a device first, then choose its cameras here. and the Add Device button.

The full-screen wall of a device

The full-screen wall shows all enabled channels of one device edge to edge. It opens from the device page and from the Live tab (see “The Full Screen Wall”), and it follows the way in which the iPhone is held: upright, the thumbnails keep their 16:9 shape and are stacked in rows of one or two (see the Layout button below); sideways, two rows fill the height. A device with only one enabled channel shows that camera over the whole screen.

  1. Tap the Full Screen Wall button (second of the three buttons) at the top right of the device page.

    Check: The wall covers the screen. It shows the enabled channels of the device; channels beyond the plan show Not in your plan. When no channel is enabled, the screen shows No Enabled Cameras and Enable cameras on the device page.

  2. When more channels exist than fit on the screen, scroll the wall vertically in upright orientation, or swipe sideways in landscape orientation.

    Check: The next channels or the next page appear. When the wall is divided into pages, dots at the bottom show the current page.

  3. Tap a thumbnail that shows a picture.

    Check: A close-up of that camera opens over the wall, with Back and the camera name at the top.

  4. Tap Back at the top left of the close-up.

    Check: The wall is shown again.

  5. Tap Close (the cross at the top left of the wall).

    Check: The screen returns to the device page or to the Live tab.
